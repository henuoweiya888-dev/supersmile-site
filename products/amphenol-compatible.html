Amphenol Cable Assemblies

Amphenol describes a broad group of interconnection technologies, not one standard cable. A project may involve a circular industrial connector, a sealed vehicle branch, a battery power link or a specialized communication channel. Super Smile develops defined assemblies from drawings, samples and confirmed mating references. This guide follows the selection from connector families through materials, application environments and manufacturing controls. It explains what the assembly needs to do and what evidence must be agreed before supply, rather than treating every catalogue option as an already qualified company product.

01

Amphenol Circular Connectors

Circular is a shape, not a universal interface. C091 signalmate, ecomate C16, ecomate RM and MIL-DTL-38999 families differ in coupling, inserts, contact systems and intended qualification. Specify the shell size, insert arrangement, contact gender and key orientation before selecting a backshell. The cable exit, panel thickness and space needed to rotate a coupling ring also matter. An assembly that mates on the bench can still be impossible to install behind a crowded panel. Identify both ends with manufacturer drawings; never infer interchangeability from diameter or pin count.

02

Battery Connectors

Battery links are selected as a power path, including the cable, compression joint, connector and equipment terminal. SurLok and SurLok Plus are useful Amphenol search terms, but they are not the same environmental configuration. The selected variant determines sealing, contact size, cable acceptance and accessory options. Continuous current, pulse duration, enclosure temperature and cooling affect the thermal review. Provide polarity, mechanical keying and an installation drawing with the enquiry. High-voltage work also requires an approved safety and validation plan; colour alone is not evidence of electrical capability.

03

Why Use an Amphenol Connection System?

A broad catalogue can make it easier to find a documented interface for a particular job. Reliability still comes from matching that interface to the environment and manufacturing it correctly. Compare quotations on a complete BOM basis: contacts, seals, locks, backshells, tooling, inspection and minimum purchasing quantities can change the total cost. A cheaper housing can become an expensive assembly if its contacts are unavailable or installation needs extra labour. We explain the selected family and proposed alternatives, without claiming that one brand is always cheaper, more reliable or suitable for every application.

04

Materials and Supply-Chain Traceability

Material control begins with identifiable part references and purchasing records. The wire specification should describe conductor construction, insulation, outer diameter and required markings, not just a colour and nominal area. Record housing and contact revisions where they affect fit or processing. Material declarations, certificates of conformity and inspection records answer different questions. Agree which documents are needed and how they relate to the supplied batch before ordering. A familiar supplier name is not a substitute for checking the actual material delivered to the line.

05

Silicone Seals and Complete Sealing Paths

Rear wire seals, interface seals, cavity plugs and panel seals protect different entry paths. Their dimensions and compression must match the specified housing and cable. A seal selected by conductor gauge alone can be wrong because two wires with the same copper area may have different insulation diameters. Chemical exposure also depends on the particular elastomer compound and fluid. Amphenol's ecomate RSSM guidance, for example, requires RSSM plugs and receptacles together for its stated IP69K performance. Do not transfer that rating to a mixed pair or a finished harness without the relevant evidence.

06

Connector Housing Materials

Thermoplastic and metal shells solve different packaging problems. A lightweight polymer housing can integrate latches and colour coding; a metal shell may support a defined shielding and mechanical interface. Neither material is automatically the better choice. Review impact, vibration, temperature, cleaning fluids, UV exposure and panel mounting together. Resin type, reinforcement and flame classification belong to the exact component specification. A UL94 classification for a plastic specimen is not a fire-survival claim for the entire cable assembly. Preserve the manufacturer's approved accessories and installation conditions.

07

Terminals: Conductor Crimp and Insulation Support

Stamped-and-formed contacts and machined contacts need their own tooling and acceptance instructions. In an open-barrel contact, the conductor crimp establishes the electrical and mechanical joint while a separate support feature may grip the insulation or seal. That support must not be confused with the conductor crimp. A machined contact may use an indentation process and a different locator. Strip length, strand damage, contact position and pull-test requirements are therefore contact-specific. There is no single universal crimp setting for all Amphenol families or wire sizes.

08

Gold-Plated Contacts: Specify the Finish

Contact plating is a controlled material stack, not merely a visible colour. Gold, tin and other finishes must match the selected contact design and mating partner. The approved drawing establishes thickness and underplate where applicable; a catalogue value from one family does not apply to every Amphenol pin. Low-level signals can be sensitive to unstable contact resistance, while power contacts need a thermal review at their actual load. Keep contact finish in the BOM and avoid unapproved mixing during a supply substitution. Mating-cycle capability is also configuration-dependent.

09

High-Voltage Connector Harnesses

A high-voltage connection can combine power contacts, touch protection, shielding and an interlock circuit, depending on the selected series. HVIL reports a connection state to the equipment's control system; it does not independently make every exposed conductor safe. The project must define voltage, current, creepage and clearance, shield termination, cable retention and the isolation procedure. High-voltage cable assemblies enter engineering and process review before a supply commitment. Any dielectric or insulation-resistance test must use approved limits and account for fitted electronics, rather than applying an arbitrary test voltage to every assembly.

10

Automotive Interfaces and Deutsch Cross-References

AT, ATM, ATP and AHD are distinct Amphenol Sine product families. Cross-reference information can help identify a candidate, but approval needs the exact original and proposed part numbers, including suffixes. Check keying, seal range, contact system, accessories and mating dimensions. ATM uses a smaller contact system than AT or ATP; the names are not synonyms. A diagnostic AHD connector also needs the agreed vehicle pin map and mechanical coding. Treat a cross-reference as the beginning of a comparison, not proof that every colour, cavity count or special variant is interchangeable.

11

Telecom Connectors and Cable Architecture

The expression 'Amphenol cable' can refer to very different products: a multipair telecom loom, an RF coaxial assembly or a high-speed copper interconnect. Clarify the interface and signal standard before discussing length. A 25-pair telecom lead needs pair mapping and termination identification; a coaxial channel needs the impedance and connector/cable combination; a high-speed link needs the defined channel and performance limits. Physical continuity cannot establish bandwidth, return loss or crosstalk. Fibre-optic and advanced high-speed work require a separate capability and measurement review rather than a generic harness quotation.

12

Secure, Compliant and Documented

Component approvals, factory quality systems and finished-assembly acceptance are separate subjects. Specify which standards and revisions apply to the order, which documents must accompany it and which tests need a dedicated plan. RoHS or REACH declarations are not electrical test reports. IPC/WHMA-A-620 addresses cable and harness workmanship where contractually adopted; it does not automatically qualify a harness for a vehicle, aircraft or space mission. Super Smile confirms the available records for the approved BOM and manufacturing scope. Brand names and reference standards on this page do not imply authorization or certification.

13

Industries: Automotive and EV

Vehicle assemblies may serve body controls, sensors, charging auxiliaries, thermal management or high-voltage power. These circuits have different electrical and safety boundaries even when they occupy the same compartment. Capture the mating device, trim or platform version, local heat, fluid exposure and attachment points. The installed route must allow service without loading terminals or bypassing protective features. A prototype should be reviewed on the target equipment, including moving parts and access for locking. Safety-related and high-voltage circuits remain subject to the customer's approved design and qualification process.

14

Industries: Aerospace and Defense

MIL-DTL-38999 identifies a connector specification family, not an automatic approval for any cable built around a circular shell. A project can require exact shell finish, insert arrangement, contact specification, shielding, identification and material traceability. The drawing may also control routing, lacing and inspection records. We first review the proposed assembly and evidence requirements; regulated or mission-critical work is not offered solely because a commercial connector appears similar. Where an application requires a qualified source or a specific manufacturing approval, that requirement must be resolved before accepting the work.

15

Industries: Space Applications

Space hardware adds concerns that ordinary outdoor ratings do not cover: vacuum outgassing, contamination of sensitive surfaces, thermal cycling and radiation exposure. NASA's materials database reports outgassing measurements such as total mass loss and collected volatile condensable material; it is a material-selection resource, not approval of a complete harness. Adhesives, sleeves, marking materials and potting can matter as much as the wire insulation. Space-related enquiries require a project-specific qualification and sourcing review. No flight heritage or space manufacturing qualification is implied by the examples discussed here.

16

Industries: Telecom and Data

Equipment rooms and outdoor cabinets can need power distribution, copper data links, coaxial feeders and optical connections in the same system. Preserve the distinction between those channels. Cable routing, bend limits, shielding and connector cleanliness affect different performance measures. Amphenol's high-speed catalogue includes specialized cable architectures that cannot be replicated by simply changing plugs on general-purpose wire. For a project enquiry, provide the interface standard, channel length, topology and required test evidence. We identify which assembly operations are within scope and which need specialist supply or validation.

17

Industries: Industrial Equipment and Robotics

An industrial harness can connect a stationary controller to moving sensors, valves, drives or an end effector. Separate fixed routing from repeated bending and torsion before selecting cable construction. Protect the connector from becoming the mechanical stop at the end of travel. Coolant, welding debris and cleaning chemicals require their own exposure review. On-machine circular connectors can make maintenance more modular, but an accessible coupling and a replaceable branch must be planned into the equipment. Movement life belongs to the tested cable-and-installation combination, not to the connector brand alone.

18

Industries: Renewable Energy

Solar, storage and wind equipment combine long service intervals with different electrical environments. A photovoltaic string connector, a battery-rack power link and a turbine control harness are not interchangeable products. Review voltage, loading, temperature rise, UV exposure, water paths and maintenance access for the actual circuit. Preserve approved mating pairs and cable dimensions, particularly where a connector's seal depends on cable diameter. We can review defined power and control assemblies; field installation and system-level electrical protection remain part of the equipment's engineering and approval process.

19

Consolidating Hybrid Power and Signal

A hybrid insert places defined power and signal contacts in one housing. Families such as ecomate C16 offer mixed arrangements, but the exact insert determines the supported circuits. Combining functions can reduce separate connection points and simplify a modular machine interface; it also concentrates more requirements in one assembly. Allocate each contact by function, then review voltage separation, current loading, shielding and the consequences of a shared disconnect. A power contact and a low-level signal contact need different cable and termination choices. Preserve pair geometry where required and document how shields are connected to the shell or equipment. Built-in shielding is not a promise of zero interference. Mechanical routing, return paths and the equipment's EMC design still matter. Compare the combined layout with separate connectors for installation time, service access and replacement cost. Any weight or time saving should be measured against the actual alternative, not quoted as a universal percentage.

20

Jackets: High Temperature, XLPE and Silicone

The hottest point is often local: near an exhaust, heater, power contact or tightly packed cable group. Review the conductor's operating temperature and the surrounding equipment separately. XLPE and silicone constructions are available for elevated-temperature duties, but their ratings depend on the complete cable specification. Insulation, jacket, connector seal and sleeve may have different limits. A heat-resistant jacket cannot compensate for an overloaded contact or a route touching a hot surface. Define continuous exposure, short excursions and abrasion protection before selecting the material.

21

Jackets: PTFE, FEP and Chemical Exposure

PTFE and FEP are fluoropolymer options used in demanding cable constructions, but 'chemical resistant' is not a complete specification. Name the chemical, concentration, temperature and duration, and distinguish occasional splashes from immersion. The jacket, core insulation, printing, adhesive and connector seal can respond differently to the same fluid. A material selected for excellent chemical behaviour may need additional mechanical protection or different termination processing. Compare the cable manufacturer's resistance information with the actual exposure; do not infer compatibility from the polymer abbreviation alone.

22

Jackets: UV and Outdoor Weathering

Outdoor exposure combines sunlight, moisture, temperature cycling and mechanical movement. Black colour by itself does not establish UV resistance, and an indoor flame-retardant cable is not automatically suitable outside. Specify a cable grade with the required weathering evidence and review markings, boots and ties as part of the same installation. Avoid layouts that hold water at cable entries or abrade the jacket against a bracket. Protective conduit can help with mechanical exposure, but it does not remove the need to consider heat build-up and drainage.

23

Jackets: Low Smoke and Halogen-Free Requirements

Low smoke, halogen content and flame propagation describe different properties and may require different test methods. IEC 61034 addresses smoke-density measurement under defined burning conditions; a result there is not a complete fire-performance classification. An LSZH or LSOH designation must be tied to the specified cable and supporting documentation. It does not mean the cable is fireproof, emits no harmful gases or keeps a circuit operating during a fire. Where such behaviour matters, identify the installation standard, test scope and acceptance evidence before choosing the jacket and other assembly materials.

24

Jackets: Flexibility in Cold Weather

Storage temperature, fixed-installation temperature and permitted movement temperature can be different. A cable that survives a cold night while stationary may be damaged if bent immediately at that temperature. Review the actual grade's minimum installation and moving limits, not just its lowest published number. Cable stiffness also changes the force applied to an entry seal or clamp. Provide the start-up temperature, motion profile and bend radius for refrigerated equipment or outdoor winter service. TPE and other low-temperature constructions must be assessed as complete cables, not selected by material name alone.

25

Jackets: High-Flex and Robotic Motion

A flexible jacket does not by itself create a continuous-flex cable. Stranding, conductor lay, fillers, shield construction and the jacket work together. Repeated linear bending and torsion impose different loads, so a drag-chain cable should not automatically be assigned to a rotating robot joint. Use the cable's specified bend factor with its actual outer diameter, and review travel, acceleration and the support arrangement. Keep termination transitions outside uncontrolled bending where possible. Any cycle-life statement must include the relevant test conditions and installation limits.

26

Technical Support: Design Review and Parts

Start with the mating references and electrical schematic, then work outward to the installed route. We review cavity assignment, current paths, cable construction, protective accessories and assembly access. A clear bill of materials distinguishes original branded parts from alternatives offered for approval. It also identifies items that are not supplied with the housing, such as contacts or backshell accessories. Open questions are resolved before tooling or material commitments. The review's scope and any associated engineering work are agreed for the project rather than advertised as an unlimited free service.

27

Technical Support: Length, Layout and Prototypes

Overall length needs a measurement datum: connector face, backshell exit, branch junction or another defined feature. A loose cable laid on a table can give a different result from the installed path. Record branch lengths, breakout angles, labels and connector orientation on the drawing. A first article checks fit, routing and electrical function before repeat production. Timing depends on part availability, tooling, engineering questions and the agreed validation. We confirm a realistic sample plan instead of promising every Amphenol configuration within a fixed number of days.

28

Technical Support: Tooling and Electrical Tests

The contact's application instruction determines the tool, locator and settings. Process checks and equipment calibration support repeatability, while final inspection checks the agreed assembly. A continuity fixture can verify mapped circuits and detect specified open, short or crossed connections. Insulation resistance, dielectric testing, shield continuity and signal performance are separate requirements with their own methods and limits. Fitted resistors, diodes or electronics can change the permitted test. The quotation and inspection plan should state which checks are included and which need additional equipment or external validation.

29

Technical Assistance After Delivery

A useful service enquiry identifies the drawing revision, affected batch, mating equipment and observed symptom. Photographs of the installed route, locking features and cable entries can distinguish a geometry problem from an electrical one. Avoid modifying a returned sample before the agreed inspection, because the original condition may be important evidence. If the design changes, update the BOM, drawing and inspection criteria together. The aim is a traceable correction that can be repeated in later production, not an undocumented adjustment made only to one replacement lead.

30

Select the Correct Amphenol Assembly

For an accurate enquiry, provide connector references at both ends, circuit mapping, wire or cable requirements, branch dimensions, installation conditions and quantities. State whether original branded components are mandatory and whether alternatives may be reviewed. Identify required documentation and any special tests before comparing prices. Availability must be checked for the complete set of housings, contacts, seals and accessories. Super Smile offers project-defined cable and harness assembly; this page is not a claim of Amphenol distributorship, universal stock or approval for every regulated application.

Engineering and Purchasing Questions

Are all Amphenol circular connectors interchangeable?

No. Match the complete series, shell and insert arrangement, contact system, keying and accessories using the approved mating references.

Does a connector rating establish the finished harness rating?

No. Cable construction, seals, termination, installation and test conditions must also be reviewed. Project evidence determines what may be claimed for the assembly.

Can one enquiry cover power, signal and hybrid options?

Yes. Provide circuit requirements and the installation layout so the options can be compared on a complete BOM, routing and validation basis.

What determines lead time and documentation?

The approved BOM, component availability, tooling, engineering questions, quantities and test plan. These are confirmed for the order rather than promised universally.
