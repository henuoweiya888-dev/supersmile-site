Low-Voltage New-Energy Harness

Overview

Low-voltage new-energy harnesses serve auxiliary controllers, sensors, pumps, fans, lighting, communication or diagnostic functions in electric or new-energy equipment. This category does not mean high-voltage traction or battery power cabling.

Product Introduction

Auxiliary power, control, sensing and communication for new-energy equipment

Low-voltage new-energy harnesses connect auxiliary controllers, sensors, interlocks, pumps, fans, lighting, service ports, communication nodes and diagnostic circuits in electric vehicles, charging equipment, energy storage, photovoltaic and other electrified systems. This category excludes high-voltage traction and battery power cabling. The project must define the voltage boundary, circuit currents, protection strategy, connector system, grounding, shielding, sealing and inspection plan for the actual platform.

Low-Voltage New-Energy Harness Types

Auxiliary controller and actuator harness

Connects low-voltage controllers, relays, contactor coils, pumps, fans and small actuators. Circuit current, inrush and protective device coordination are defined by the platform.

Sensor and monitoring harness

Links temperature, pressure, position, current and other sensors to monitoring or control modules with the required reference ground and signal routing.

Pump, fan and thermal-management harness

Supports low-voltage circulation, cooling and ventilation equipment through sealed branches, controlled current paths and serviceable connectors.

Communication and diagnostic harness

Carries CAN, serial, Ethernet or other project-defined communication and service circuits with controlled pin mapping, pair structure and shielding where required.

Interlock and safety-related signal harness

Connects access switches, state feedback and defined interlock circuits. Functional safety claims remain a system-level responsibility and require project-specific review.

Lighting, HMI and service accessory harness

Organizes low-voltage lighting, displays, switches, maintenance ports and auxiliary accessories into branches matched to the equipment layout.

Electrical and Material Recommendations

Conductor size and current path

Continuous current, inrush, voltage drop, ambient temperature, bundle derating, length and terminal range determine conductor construction and size.

Insulation, jacket and colour identification

Material is chosen for the platform temperature, flexing, oils, coolants, sunlight or cleaning exposure. Colour and labels support assembly but do not replace pin control.

Connector, seal and keying system

Housing, terminal, seal, cavity plug, lock, wire diameter and mating interface form one compatible system. Keying and colour should follow the equipment architecture.

Fuse, relay, grounding and shielding elements

Protection and switching components, ground returns, twisted pairs, shields and drain paths are defined with the controller and power-distribution design.

Low-Voltage Harness Solutions

Separate low-voltage scope from high-voltage scope

Nominal and maximum voltage, isolation boundaries, interfaces and applicable customer rules are confirmed before quotation. High-voltage traction requests enter a separate capability review.

Develop from system architecture and pin data

Controller I/O, power distribution, fuse strategy, loads, communication buses, grounding and service interfaces are converted into a controlled harness drawing.

Coordinate power and sensitive signals

Branch geometry, pair structure, separation, shield termination and reference grounds are arranged so switching circuits do not unnecessarily disturb sensing or communication.

Build prototypes and controlled production looms

Prototype feedback is converted into approved pin maps, BOMs, labels, work instructions and inspection points before recurring production.

New-Energy Applications

Electric and electrified mobile equipment

Supports auxiliary controls, sensing, lighting, communication and service functions outside the high-voltage traction circuit.

Charging equipment auxiliaries

Connects low-voltage controllers, indicators, locks, fans, sensors and communication modules in charging cabinets or interfaces.

Battery and energy-storage monitoring

Supports low-voltage sensing, BMS communication, service and auxiliary-control branches while the project defines voltage and isolation boundaries.

Photovoltaic and power-conversion controls

Links monitoring boards, fans, indicators, sensors and service interfaces around inverters and control enclosures, excluding their high-energy power paths.

New-energy industrial equipment

Connects auxiliary power distribution, controls, interlocks, pumps and monitoring in electrified production and process equipment.

Thermal-management and environmental systems

Routes sensors, pumps, valves and fans for defined cooling, heating and environmental-control functions.

Low-Voltage Harness Making Method

  1. Platform and voltage-boundary review

    Confirm nominal and maximum voltage, circuit list, loads, controllers, isolation boundaries, installation zones, quantities and applicable specifications.

  2. Circuit and interface definition

    Create pin maps, current paths, fuse and relay logic, grounds, communication pairs, branches, connector views and service labels.

  3. Material and routing review

    Select conductors, insulation, connectors, seals, shielding and protection around current, temperature, movement and environmental exposure.

  4. Controlled assembly

    Prepare, crimp, splice, insert, seal, route, label and protect the harness according to the approved drawing and process setup.

  5. Electrical and workmanship inspection

    Check continuity, polarity, circuit mapping, locks, seals, labels and dimensions plus other project-defined electrical items.

  6. Sample approval and production release

    Approved pin data, BOM, drawing, labels, work instructions and inspection plan become the controlled reference for recurring builds.

Electrical and Environmental Review

Movement, vibration and connector loading

Routing, bend radius, branch points, clips and service loops are reviewed so vibration and cable mass do not overload terminals or seals.

Moisture, contamination and chemicals

Connector sealing, jackets, labels and protective coverings are matched to the stated zone and exposure; new-energy use alone does not define the environment.

Power distribution and circuit protection

Continuous and transient current, fuse coordination, connector capacity, voltage drop and grounding are reviewed as one low-voltage architecture.

Communication and diagnostic integrity

Pair geometry, termination, shielding, grounds and branch topology follow the controller specifications and agreed validation method.

What a Defined Low-Voltage Architecture Improves

Clearer service and diagnostic access

Defined branches, labels, connectors and service loops make auxiliary circuits easier to identify and replace.

Fewer interface and pin-mapping errors

Controlled connector views, keying, cavity data and inspection points reduce ambiguity across engineering and assembly.

Better coordination of power and signals

Current paths, sensitive circuits, communication pairs, shielding and grounding are developed together instead of as isolated wires.

A production-ready auxiliary harness system

Drawings, BOMs, work instructions, labels and inspection plans turn a platform architecture into repeatable low-voltage assemblies.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does this category include high-voltage battery or traction cables?

No. It covers project-defined low-voltage auxiliary power, control, sensing, communication and diagnostic circuits. High-voltage requests require separate capability and compliance review.

How is the low-voltage boundary defined?

The customer provides nominal and maximum voltage, platform rules, circuit list and isolation boundaries. The label low voltage is not assumed from connector size or application name.

Can power and communication circuits share one harness?

They may when current paths, separation, pair structure, shielding, grounding and connector capacity are reviewed for the actual controller architecture.

What data is needed for development?

Provide the system architecture, voltage and current by circuit, pin maps, connector references, loads, communication type, fuse strategy, installation zone, quantities and applicable customer specifications.

Can you build from an existing low-voltage sample?

A sample can support dimensions and circuit mapping, but electrical ratings, hidden protection devices, mating interfaces and platform requirements still require confirmation.

Which checks are performed before shipment?

Finished harnesses receive continuity and appearance inspection plus agreed checks for polarity, circuit mapping, locks, seals, labels and other project-defined electrical items.

Configuration & Project Review

01 Low-voltage scope and circuit list

Identify auxiliary supply, sensing, communication and service circuits with their nominal and maximum voltages. Keep the traction and battery main-power paths outside this low-voltage definition.

02 BMS interface and pin definition

Record the controller and battery interface revisions, connector views and signal assignments. Type A and Type B BMS cables are separate pin configurations and need the correct system documentation.

03 Communication pair and shield routing

Define CAN or other communication pairs, reference ground and shield termination from the actual interface specification. Keep high-current switching branches away from sensitive signal routes where the layout requires it.
