Circular Connector Assembly

Overview

Circular connector cable assemblies cover threaded, bayonet, push-pull, panel-mount, inline, sealed and mixed-contact interfaces for power, signal, communication and sensor circuits. Each assembly is defined by its exact connector family, keying, contact arrangement, cable, backshell and installation environment.

CONNECTOR ATLAS

Circular Connector Cable Assemblies

Circular connector is a category, not a single part. We develop complete cable assemblies around the customer's mating equipment, including power-only, signal-only, shielded communication, sensor, hybrid and sealed configurations. Selection begins with the exact mechanical interface and contact map, then extends through conductor, cable, shielding, sealing, strain relief, installation and verification.

01

COUPLING 01

Bayonet and quick-turn coupling

A limited rotation seats and retains the plug, reducing connection time where repeated access is expected.

Lock position

Slots, pins, spring preload and final rotation must reach the defined position. A connector that feels tight may still be incompletely seated.

Service access

Hand clearance, visibility and cable direction are reviewed so technicians can lock and release the coupling without twisting the cable.

02

COUPLING 02

Threaded circular assemblies

Threaded coupling progressively draws the plug and receptacle together and can provide positive retention with controlled seal compression.

More than thread diameter

Thread form, engagement length, shell size, keys and insert depth all require confirmation. Being able to start the thread does not prove compatibility.

Cross-threading control

Straight alignment, accessible coupling surfaces and a defined tightening method reduce damaged threads and partially mated contacts.

03

COUPLING 03

Push-pull and self-latching interfaces

Axial mating and a release sleeve support frequent handling in instruments, test systems and compact equipment.

Keying before force

The key and alignment marks must be identified before mating. Forcing a misaligned insert can damage contacts or the insulator.

Release the sleeve

Disconnection force belongs on the release mechanism, not the cable. Rear grip and service loop are designed around the real handling direction.

04

INSTALLATION

Panel receptacles and cable plugs

Panel thickness, fixing method, rear clearance, mating depth and connector orientation are treated as one mechanical interface.

Flange and jam-nut mounting

Mounting geometry must prevent rotation and preserve access to keys, latches and rear termination hardware.

Inline connections

Without panel support, suspended mass, pull load, fixation points and backshell strain relief become part of the harness design.

05

CONTACT MAP

Contact arrangement and cavity control

Shell size alone cannot identify the electrical interface. Every cavity is mapped to function, contact sex, terminal type and conductor range.

Pins, sockets and viewing direction

Pin tables state whether the view is the mating face or wire side and whether contacts are pins or sockets, preventing mirrored wiring.

Power, signal and hybrid layouts

Large power contacts, small signal contacts, coaxial modules or paired communication circuits require separate conductor and inspection rules.

06

POLARIZATION

Keying is an electrical safeguard

Primary keys, secondary keys and coded inserts prevent incompatible circuits from being connected in the same equipment family.

Do not identify by colour alone

Colour can assist operation but does not replace series, coding, contact map and mating-part verification.

Photograph the interface correctly

A useful reference set includes the mating face, side profile, key location, markings and the equipment receptacle, all in focus.

07

TERMINATION

Crimp, solder and board termination

The connector series defines permitted contacts and tools; the cable construction determines how each conductor reaches that contact.

Crimp control

Terminal, conductor size, strand structure, strip length, applicator and inspection criteria are matched as one process.

Solder heat management

Solder quantity, heat input, wicking and insulation recession are controlled to protect flexible conductors and contact alignment.

08

REAR HARDWARE

Backshell, exit angle and strain relief

Straight and angled rear hardware change installation space, bend path, cable clamp position and service access.

Clamp the jacket, not the conductors

A correct clamp transfers pull and bending into the backshell. Too little grip loads the contacts; too much can crush the jacket.

Lock the clocking

For angled exits, clocking is frozen on the drawing so the cable clears nearby walls, covers and moving parts after installation.

09

SHIELD PATH

Metal shells do not create shielding by themselves

Shield performance depends on the continuous path from cable braid through rear hardware and shell to the panel and system ground.

Termination geometry follows frequency

A 360-degree clamp, drain wire or another method is selected from signal frequency, cable design and grounding architecture, not appearance.

Continuity is only one check

Low-frequency continuity cannot by itself demonstrate high-frequency shielding, impedance control or EMC performance.

10

SEALING SYSTEM

Ingress protection belongs to the complete mated assembly

Interface seals, cable seals, cavity plugs, cable diameter, backshell and mounting orientation work together only in the defined mated state.

Seal compression

Incomplete thread engagement, an unlocked bayonet or a displaced seal changes compression and can invalidate the intended protection.

Test the delivered configuration

Any ingress statement is tied to a defined specimen, mating part, cable, mounting direction, method and acceptance limit.

11

MATERIAL SYSTEM

Shell, insert, contacts and seals age together

Plastic, aluminium, stainless steel, plating, insulators and elastomers are selected against weight, corrosion, temperature, chemicals, wear and electrical duty.

Contact plating

Signal level, current, mating cycles and environment govern the contact system. Plating cannot be identified reliably from colour alone.

Cable jacket and protection

PVC, PUR, TPE, rubber, braid and conduit solve different flexibility, abrasion and environmental problems and are reviewed per project.

12

APPLICATION 01

Audio, control and stage interconnects

Round locking connectors can support balanced audio, lighting control and equipment links where frequent mating, cable handling and clear channel identification matter.

Circuit assignment

A familiar connector shape may carry different signals. Pin assignment, shielding and equipment responsibility are confirmed before production.

13

APPLICATION 02

Data and legacy equipment interfaces

Circular data interfaces remain common in installed equipment, controllers and instruments, where exact pin maps matter more than connector familiarity.

Replication from samples

A sample can establish dimensions and visible wiring, but hidden circuitry, ratings and proprietary responsibility still require customer confirmation.

14

APPLICATION 03

Compact power and instrument leads

Small circular interfaces fit constrained panels and portable equipment but leave less tolerance for keying errors, bend stress and cable-exit interference.

Space review

Mating length, finger access, minimum bend region and nearby controls are checked using the final equipment layout.

15

ENGINEERING DATA

What we need to build the correct assembly

A precise interface package shortens sample cycles and prevents similar-looking but incompatible connector selections.

Interface and circuits

Provide connector family, mating part, keying, contact layout, pin map, voltage, current, signal type and shielding expectation.

Cable and installation

Provide cable length and construction, panel details, exit direction, fixation points, temperature, vibration, moisture, chemicals, quantities and validation method.

16

BUILD AND VERIFY

From pin map to repeat production

The approved interface, cable, assembly sequence and inspection plan become one controlled manufacturing reference.

Build sequence

Prepare conductors, crimp or solder, inspect the termination, insert contacts, fit seals, terminate shielding and assemble the backshell in the specified order.

Verification

Check continuity, polarity, cavities, shorts, mating, locking, dimensions and appearance, then perform agreed pull, resistance, insulation, shielding, ingress, vibration or flex tests.

Circular Connector Questions

Can circular connectors with the same diameter be interchanged?

No. Family, keying, contact arrangement, shell fit, coupling, contacts and seals can differ. Compatibility requires complete interface data and mating verification.

Does a metal circular connector automatically provide shielding?

No. Shielding depends on cable shield termination, backshell, shell continuity, panel connection and system grounding. A metal appearance alone proves nothing.

Are all circular connector assemblies waterproof?

No. Protection depends on the complete mated interface, seals, cavity plugs, backshell, cable diameter, mounting orientation and the defined test configuration.

Can you develop from an existing sample?

A sample can support dimensional and circuit mapping, but hidden ratings, material, mating-part data, shielding strategy and intellectual-property responsibility still require customer confirmation.

How do you choose between crimp and solder contacts?

The connector series, conductor range, production volume, service method, vibration and process requirements govern the choice. Both methods need defined tools, controls and inspection.

What is checked before shipment?

Finished assemblies receive continuity and appearance inspection plus agreed checks for polarity, cavities, shorts, mating, locking, dimensions, labels, terminal retention, seals and shield treatment. Other tests follow the approved plan.
