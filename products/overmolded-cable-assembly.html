Overmolded Cable Assembly

Overview

Overmolded cable assemblies encapsulate the connector-to-cable transition with a selected thermoplastic or elastomer to provide defined strain relief, sealing, touch protection, geometry and appearance. The result depends on the complete interface, cable jacket, molding material, insert preparation and process window.

Product Introduction

Connector protection, strain relief and molded geometry developed as one system

Overmolded cable assemblies cover far more than one connector style. They include straight or angled cable exits, soft strain-relief boots, hard shells, sealed junctions, molded branches, PCB or sensor encapsulation and two-shot constructions. The electrical termination is completed first, then the insert, cable jacket and molding resin are combined through a controlled tool and process. Material labels alone cannot predict adhesion, sealing or flex life, so geometry, preparation and validation must follow the actual assembly.

Overmolded Assembly Configurations

Molded connector and cable exit

A molded body stabilizes the termination and shapes a straight, side or angled cable exit. The design controls wall thickness, lock access, mating clearance and bend transition.

Soft strain-relief boot

Flexible ribs or a tapered boot distribute bending away from the termination. Hardness, rib spacing, jacket grip and free cable length are tuned for the intended movement.

Hard-shell overmolded connector

A rigid molded body can create handling surfaces, polarization, mounting features or touch protection while preserving the original mating interface and tolerances.

Molded splice and branch junction

Splices, electronic components or multi-cable breakouts can be encapsulated when cavity filling, heat input, wire position and serviceability are reviewed.

Low-pressure electronic encapsulation

Sensitive boards, sensors or modules may use a low-pressure molding route to reduce component loading. Resin, board cleanliness, connector masking and heat exposure remain project specific.

Pre-mold plus final overmold

A hard or stabilizing pre-mold can secure terminals, shields and components before the final cosmetic or flexible layer is applied. The two materials need a compatible interface.

Molding and Cable Material Recommendations

Conductors, terminals and internal terminations

Current, voltage drop, signal function and crimp or solder geometry are confirmed before molding because the finished termination may no longer be accessible for correction.

Insert, shell and mechanical locking

Metal and plastic inserts need retention features, clean surfaces and controlled clearances. Knurls, undercuts, holes or pre-mold geometry can provide mechanical locking when chemical adhesion is limited.

TPE, TPU, PVC, PA and low-pressure resins

Material selection considers hardness, melt temperature, shrinkage, abrasion, oils, chemicals, UV, flammability and compatibility with the insert and cable jacket. No resin is universal.

Cable jacket and thermal compatibility

The cable jacket must tolerate molding heat and pressure without softening, distortion or migration. Surface energy and additives can also change adhesion to the overmold.

Overmolding Engineering Solutions

Design from the interface and duty cycle

Mating clearance, user handling, cable pull direction, bending, sealing, chemicals and assembly space define the molded envelope before a tool is selected.

Review existing tooling without forcing a fit

An existing tool may reduce development work only when insert position, cable diameter, interface clearance, gate, parting line and performance requirements genuinely match.

Control insert position and cavity filling

Fixtures support connectors, cables, shields and components against injection movement. Gate and vent locations are arranged to fill thin sections without trapping air or displacing wires.

Validate the molded assembly, not just its appearance

Dimensions, continuity, mating, cable pull, bending, section cuts, sealing or environmental tests are selected according to the actual failure risks and agreed acceptance limits.

Industries and Applications

Industrial sensors and actuators

Overmolding can protect compact interfaces and cable exits on sensors, valves, switches and actuators exposed to handling, vibration or contamination.

Automation and production equipment

Molded connectors provide repeatable handling and strain relief for equipment modules, fixtures and replaceable station cables.

Machine tools and service cables

Defined boots and rigid bodies protect connectors around oil, chips, vibration and frequent technician handling when materials are matched to the exposure.

Robotics and moving equipment

A tuned strain-relief transition can manage repeated cable movement, but flex life still depends on the cable, routing, bend radius and attachment system.

Appliances and commercial equipment

Molded junctions and plugs can combine touch protection, recognizable geometry, cleanability and repeatable assembly around motors, heaters and controls.

Instrumentation and test systems

Custom exits, shield terminations and compact molded bodies support probes, instruments and fixtures when signal requirements and grounding are defined.

Overmolded Cable Making Method

  1. Requirement and interface review

    Confirm electrical function, interfaces, cable, molded geometry, environment, loads, colour, quantity, validation and any tooling constraints.

  2. Drawing and material definition

    Define insert datums, cable exit, parting line, gate-sensitive faces, wall thickness, strain-relief profile, resin and inspection dimensions.

  3. Electrical termination and pre-mold preparation

    Complete crimping, soldering, splicing, shield termination and electrical checks, then stabilize and clean the insert for molding.

  4. Tool setup and first-off molding

    Set insert fixtures, temperature, pressure, speed, cooling and venting, then inspect short shots, flash, sink, cable movement and deformation on first pieces.

  5. Dimensional, electrical and mechanical validation

    Check molded dimensions, mating, continuity, polarity and appearance plus agreed pull, bend, section, ingress or environmental items.

  6. Process release and repeat production

    Release the approved drawing, BOM, tool setup, sample, work instruction and inspection plan as one controlled production reference.

Molding and Reliability Review

Cable pull, bending and strain transfer

Pull load and repeated bending can concentrate at the conductor termination if the boot is too stiff, too short or poorly bonded to the jacket.

Chemical, moisture and jacket interaction

Oils, cleaners, water and plasticizer migration can change resin, jacket and interface behavior. Compatibility needs representative materials and conditioning.

Internal electrical integrity after molding

Heat, pressure and insert movement can disturb terminals, solder joints, shields or components, so post-mold electrical checks remain mandatory.

Sealing and performance claim boundaries

Overmolding can support sealing, but any ingress, temperature, flame or endurance claim requires a defined specimen, method and acceptance limit.

What Controlled Overmolding Improves

More stable connector-to-cable transition

Controlled molded geometry distributes handling and bending loads while protecting the internal termination from direct contact.

Repeatable form and assembly orientation

Tool-controlled exits, keys, grips and mounting features reduce assembly ambiguity across repeated builds.

Integrated protection around compact electronics

Suitable processes can combine insulation, component support, touch protection and environmental covering within a compact envelope.

A controlled molded cable product

Electrical assembly, material set, tool, process settings, dimensions and inspection points are released together instead of treating molding as decoration.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between overmolding and using a separate boot?

Overmolding forms material directly around the insert and cable in a tool, creating a controlled integrated geometry. A separate boot is assembled afterward and may not provide the same retention, sealing or dimensional control.

Which molding material should be used?

The choice depends on hardness, flexing, temperature, chemicals, abrasion, flame requirements, colour, cable-jacket compatibility and processing temperature. A material name alone is not enough.

Can an existing mold be reused?

Possibly, after insert position, cable diameter, interface clearance, exit direction, parting line, gate and required performance are compared. Similar appearance does not guarantee fit.

Does overmolding automatically make a cable waterproof?

No. Sealing depends on the complete interface, material compatibility, cable jacket, insert geometry, molding quality, mating condition and the agreed test configuration.

What information is required for quotation?

Provide connector and insert data, pin map, cable construction and diameter, molded dimensions, exit direction, material or hardness target, environment, quantity and required validation. A physical sample can support review.

Which inspections are performed before shipment?

Finished assemblies receive continuity and appearance inspection plus dimensional, mating, lock, flash, deformation and other agreed checks. Pull, bend, ingress or environmental tests follow the approved project plan.
