What Is a DEUTSCH Harness?

DEUTSCH, a TE Connectivity brand, covers multiple connection systems rather than a single waterproof plug. A harness may combine compact sensor branches, auxiliary power leads, panel interfaces and service disconnects, each with a different duty. Super Smile can review the cable assembly from your drawing, sample or confirmed mating references. We specify the requested component combination and distinguish original branded parts from any proposed alternative on the approved BOM. Brand names describe interfaces and do not imply authorization or endorsement. The guide below follows the series, application, lead-time and pricing topics, with additional assembly and sealing checks for practical procurement.

01

DT Series · General Equipment Connections

DEUTSCH DT is a sealed connection family used for cable-to-cable, cable-to-board and cable-to-device arrangements. The familiar DT range uses size 16 contacts, with common 2-, 3-, 4-, 6-, 8- and 12-cavity layouts. These numbers describe different things: contact size is not the wire's AWG and cavity count does not define the electrical pinout. A complete specification includes both housing references, keying, seal option, contacts and wedgelocks. DT04 and DT06 are useful search prefixes, but their remaining characters matter. Confirm the full part number and mating drawing rather than ordering by gray color or a two-pin photograph.

02

DTM Series · Compact Signal Branches

DTM uses the smaller size 20 contact system and is intended for lower-current multi-circuit connections. It can be relevant to sensors, switches and compact control branches where the approved equipment interface calls for it. It is not a miniature DT that can accept DT terminals or mate to a DT housing. Review conductor size and insulation diameter separately: the rear seal must fit the insulation while the contact barrel must fit the conductor. Choose the required cavity count and locking accessories, then define branch length, cable restraint and service access. A smaller housing should not be used to reduce space without checking the complete electrical and mechanical duty.

03

DTP Series · Power Branches

DTP supports size 12 contacts in two- and four-cavity arrangements. TE lists a 25 A contact capability for this system, but that figure is not a blanket rating for any assembled cable. Wire size, ambient temperature, simultaneously loaded circuits, termination quality and the installation route still require review. A fan, pump or auxiliary actuator may have a starting current above its steady load; record both before defining the harness. Specify the mating pair and protection strategy, not only a larger conductor. Keep unsupported cable weight and sharp bends away from the rear seal. A power connection also needs an agreed method for safe isolation during servicing.

04

DTHD Series · Single-Circuit Disconnects

DTHD is a single-terminal, circular thermoplastic connector family that can provide a serviceable alternative to a permanent splice. TE identifies versions for contact sizes 12, 8 and 4; those versions do not share one universal current or cable range. Begin with the selected mating parts and contact specification, then determine conductor cross-section, insulation diameter, cable support and available installation space. A single pole is only one circuit conductor: the return path and circuit protection must still be defined elsewhere in the system. DTHD is not automatically a high-voltage connector simply because it is used for a higher-current branch.

05

HDP20 / HD30 · Circular Multi-Pin Interfaces

HDP20 uses a thermoplastic shell, while HD30 uses an aluminum shell. The family name is only the start of an identification process: shell size, insert arrangement, contact sizes, key position, pin or socket configuration and mounting details must be matched. These interfaces can consolidate several circuits in equipment or vehicle harnesses, sometimes with more than one contact size within the selected arrangement. Do not confuse every round Deutsch connector with the nine-pin J1939 diagnostic interface. A bulkhead version also requires the panel opening, gasket, fasteners and cable exit to be considered. The complete insert drawing is more useful than a front photograph alone.

06

Automotive Applications · Engine Bay

Engine-bay wiring can encounter localized heat, vibration, oil exposure and restricted maintenance access. Measure the intended route relative to hot surfaces, pulleys and service points instead of assigning one temperature to the whole bay. A sealed connector does not protect cable insulation from an unsuitable heat source or continuous abrasion. Define clamps, sleeves, breakout locations and connector orientation as part of the harness. Sensors and actuators may require different conductor sizes and shielding arrangements. Any customer-required environmental validation should specify the actual assembly, exposure, test sequence and acceptance criteria; a connector catalog alone is not the test report for a new engine loom.

07

Automotive Applications · Chassis Systems

Underbody and off-highway harnesses face stone impact, mud, standing water and movement between supported structures. Locate disconnect points where the latch can be reached and the cable will not collect damaging loads. A branch to a suspension sensor needs enough travel for the full mechanical range, without rubbing against tires or sharp brackets. Design the connector and cable protection together: boots, conduit and clips have different functions from the interfacial seal. For braking or other safety-related circuits, use the customer-approved configuration and validation requirements. Neither a rugged photograph nor a familiar housing shape establishes safety-system suitability.

08

Automotive Applications · Exterior Lighting

Work lamps, marker lights and auxiliary lighting can use detachable sealed branches where specified by the equipment design. Confirm voltage, polarity, current, connector key and cavity assignment at each lamp. A two-way lighting lead may carry only supply and return, while a larger interface can include dimming or control lines. Check cable voltage drop over the installed length and keep the harness away from hot lamp bodies or sharp mounting plates. Lens condensation and connector water entry are different failure paths. A watertight plug will not correct an unsealed lamp enclosure or poor cable entry elsewhere in the lighting assembly.

09

Seals, Wedgelocks and Unused Cavities

The rear wire seal works over a defined insulation-diameter range; choosing the correct AWG alone does not guarantee sealing. N and E seal options, where offered, must be checked against the actual wire. Wedgelocks support contact alignment and retention but do not replace the primary contact engagement. Unused positions need the manufacturer-specified cavity treatment, not an improvised short wire. Dust caps, backshells and heat-shrink boots are accessories with distinct functions and approval conditions. Confirm any IP rating on the exact connector version, mated condition and required rear protection. Immersion, road spray and pressure washing are different exposures and should not be treated as equivalent claims.

10

Contact Preparation and Assembly Checks

Solid and stamped-and-formed contacts use different crimp geometries and tooling. Match the contact part number to the approved conductor range, strip length, tool and positioner or die before production. Inspect conductor capture, insulation clearance and contact damage against the relevant application instruction; do not transplant a crimp setting from a similar-looking terminal. After insertion, confirm cavity position and retention before closing the secondary lock. The electrical check should use the agreed pin map, including intentional splices or blank positions. Record dimensional and inspection results for the first article, then preserve that approved configuration for repeat batches.

11

Lead Times and Project Preparation

Availability depends on the complete bill of materials, including small accessories that are easy to omit from an initial enquiry. Housings may be obtainable while the required contact, seal version or mounting clip has a different lead time. Separate material confirmation, tooling preparation, sample assembly, customer approval and repeat production when planning a schedule. Supplying both mating references, circuit tables and clear dimensional datums helps reduce clarification cycles. We confirm a project-specific delivery plan after reviewing quantities and sourcing; this page does not promise that every DEUTSCH variation is stocked or available within a fixed number of days.

12

Pricing and Cost Control

A useful quotation describes the exact assembly, not just a price per connector. Cost drivers include the specified brand and part references, contact type and finish, wire construction, branch count, labeling, protection, tooling, order quantity and verification scope. Distinguish original specified parts from an approved alternative so prices are compared on equal terms. Simplifying a branch or selecting a serviceable disconnect can reduce assembly effort, but any change needs interface and performance review. Supply annual demand and initial sample quantity separately. The quotation should identify included inspection records and any separately scoped environmental or specialized electrical testing.

Selection and Assembly Questions

Can DT, DTM and DTP mate with each other?

No general interchangeability should be assumed. They use different contact systems and housing geometries. Match the exact mating references, keying and contact requirements.

Does a contact size of 16 mean 16 AWG wire?

No. Contact size identifies the interface system. Each contact part has its own conductor range, while the rear seal has an insulation-diameter range. Confirm all three together.

Are all Deutsch harnesses rated for pressure washing?

No blanket claim is appropriate. Verify the exact connector version, mating state, rear protection and the required test conditions. A general waterproof description is not a pressure-wash qualification.

What information helps identify an existing connector?

Clear front and rear photos, molded markings, cavity count, key orientation, wire dimensions and the mating component are useful. A confirmed drawing or full part number is preferable to color alone.

Provide full mating references, wire sizes and insulation diameters, pin map, dimensions, operating conditions and quantities.

03 Release Requirements

Approve the component combination, tooling and first article before releasing repeat assembly and agreed verification.

04 Mating & Termination

Identify the mating part number and termination method before confirming the assembly drawing.

05 Sample Review

Compare the sample with the approved pin map, dimensions and material list.
