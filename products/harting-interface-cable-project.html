Harting Interface Cable Project

Overview

Cable assemblies built around a specified HARTING Han interface for removable machines, control cabinets and equipment modules. The complete part references, mating half, circuit drawing and installation duty define the assembly; similarly named hoods, inserts and contacts are not automatically interchangeable.

HOOD / INSERT / CONTACT / CABLE

A Han interface is a system, not just a plug

Start at the equipment boundary: which side stays fixed, which side disconnects, and where power, control and data circuits go. Then specify hood, base, insert or modules, contacts, seals and locks from the manufacturer's documentation. Images illustrate related structures and contexts; only part-number-verified components form a project configuration.

Interface architecture and modules

HOOD & MATING

Specify the complete hood, housing and latch combination

A rectangular heavy-duty interface works as a system of cable hood, equipment housing, insert, locking hardware and cable entry. The same housing size does not guarantee the same latch position, exit direction, mounting holes or protection level. Check full part references and mating-face views on both sides; document top/side entry, single/double latch and mounting and service clearances. INSERT OR MODULES

A monobloc insert is not a visual substitute for Han-Modular

Han E, Han D, Han DD and Han-Modular follow different configuration paths. A modular system can arrange power, signal or data in approved frame-and-module combinations, but each module has its own contact form, position, wire range and rating. The pictured controls show coexisting equipment circuits, not Han modules. Group circuits by load, separation and signal integrity first; then select inserts or modules using the manufacturer's configuration, size and rating data. A common hood alone does not authorize a mixed build.

Circuit first, module second

Review power, control, data and shielding separately.

Make service traceable

Map frame positions and module references to the drawing.

Termination, cable entry and protection

WIRE PREPARATION

Cutting, termination and contact retention are separate checks

Choose conductor cross-section, strip length and method from the approved contact and wire range. Han variants use different crimp, screw or spring-cage terminations; tooling and instructions cannot be copied across contacts. The cutting photo illustrates preparation only, not a crimp or project inspection record. After termination, check exposed conductor, insulation position, full contact seating and retention during mating. Continuity cannot replace a termination-quality or retention check.

Specify termination method

Match contact number, tool and conductor range.

Record checks separately

ENTRY & SEAL

Match gland, jacket and bend relief to the cable

Housing protection cannot be assessed apart from cable entry and mated condition. The selected gland's clamping range must match the actual jacket diameter; seals and jacket must suit the installation, oil, dust, cleaning and temperature exposure. The photo shows a disassembled generic cable gland and seal, not specified Han parts. For a movable module, document fixing points, bend radius, pull risk and service loop so the latch and contacts do not become strain-relief devices.

Measure the actual jacket

Assess the complete assembly

Installation and first-article release

EQUIPMENT BOUNDARY

Removable machines and enclosures need different routing

A Han interface may sit at the boundary between a removable module and fixed wiring, but the position determines the cable design. Machine modules require mating access, latch operation, movement allowance and mis-mating prevention; enclosures and outdoor equipment add cable-entry direction, water path, support and service access. Ask the customer to mark the fixed half, mating direction, cable support and worst-case service position before setting length and exit angle.

FIRST-ARTICLE REVIEW

Inspect against the approved mating half, not a cable alone

A used sample helps identify size and cable exit, but may hide substituted inserts or field repairs. Check the first article against the approved BOM and circuit drawing: module position, pins, wire IDs, contact seating, latch closure, cable-entry retention, length and continuity; mate with the approved opposite half when available. Harting interface questions

Is the Han housing size enough to specify a cable?

No. Confirm both hoods and housings, insert or modules, contacts, latches, cable entry, pinout and cable specification.

Can Han-Modular directly replace a monobloc insert?

Not by size alone. The frame, cavities, contacts, load and separation conditions need a new engineering review and customer approval.

Does the hood's IP marking rate the entire cable assembly?

No. Actual protection depends on the specified housing, seals, gland, cable diameter, assembly and properly mated condition; confirm the project claim against exact data and tests.

Can one interface carry power and data?

Han-Modular offers combination paths, but a mixed build depends on approved modules, spacing, ratings, shielding and the project's electrical-separation requirements.

What information matters most for a quotation?

Send complete part references or clear mating-face photos of both halves, pin map, loads, wire and length, exit direction, environment, quantity and acceptance criteria. If references are unresolved, start with an interface review rather than promising compatibility.
