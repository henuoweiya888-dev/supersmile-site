RF & Coaxial Assembly

Overview

RF and coaxial cable assemblies carry radio-frequency, microwave, video, timing and antenna signals through a controlled-impedance path. This category includes flexible, low-loss, miniature, semi-rigid, panel-mount and application-specific assemblies—not one universal coax lead.

Specify the Channel Before the Cable

Impedance, loss, shielding, connectors and installed RF performance

A useful RF cable is not selected from a connector photograph. We define the operating band, impedance, loss budget, return path, connector reference plane and installed environment first; cable family and termination process follow those inputs.

    RF and Coaxial Assembly Questions

    Can an RF cable be selected from connector photos?

    No. Photos help identify a family, but exact interface, gender, polarity, impedance, frequency, cable type, length and environment still require drawings, part numbers or equipment data.

    What is the difference between 50-ohm and 75-ohm coax?

    They use different conductor and dielectric geometry and serve different systems. Fifty ohms is common in RF and test equipment; 75 ohms is common in video and broadcast. The cable and both interfaces must match the equipment impedance.

    Does a higher-frequency connector guarantee the complete cable rating?

    No. The usable assembly band is limited by the cable, both connectors, termination quality, transitions, length, routing and acceptance limits. The complete assembly must be reviewed and, where required, measured.

    Can you build phase-matched RF cable sets?

    The project can be reviewed when frequency band, number of channels, nominal length, phase or delay tolerance, bend state, connector planes and test method are defined. Capability and limits are confirmed before quotation.

    Is continuity testing enough for an RF assembly?

    Continuity detects open and short circuits but does not measure insertion loss, mismatch, phase, shielding or PIM. The application determines which additional RF tests and limits are needed.

    What should be supplied for a custom quotation?

    Send mating connector part numbers or drawings, impedance, frequency band, length, cable and exit preference, power or bias, loss and VSWR targets, shielding or PIM requirement, environment, quantity and validation method. A physical sample can supplement but not replace electrical definition.
