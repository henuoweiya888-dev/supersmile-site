Jumper Harness

Overview

Jumper harnesses are short interconnects used between boards, modules, terminal points or adjacent devices. Their simple appearance can hide different pin routes, orientation and current requirements.

Ask About This Product TypeBack to Products

Product Introduction

Short interconnections with exact pin routing

A jumper harness may be only a few centimetres long, but it is not a generic patch lead. This category includes board-to-board jumpers, terminal jumpers, adapter leads, crossed pin routes, split circuits, service links and short power or signal looms. The correct result depends on both mating interfaces, pin mapping, current or signal function, insertion direction, length datum and the way the jumper is installed and serviced.

Jumper Harness Configurations

Board-to-board and module jumpers

Short multi-core links between a main PCB, display, relay board, sensor module or daughterboard. Connector pitch, keying, pin numbering, insertion direction and bend space must match both ends.

Terminal block and panel jumpers

Pre-cut links for adjacent or repeated terminal points, switches, breakers and panel devices. Length, ferrules or terminals, colour and numbering are coordinated with the panel layout.

Connector adapter jumpers

Converts one connector family, pitch or contact arrangement to another without changing the required electrical function. Both mating part numbers and a confirmed pin table are essential.

Straight-through, crossed and polarity jumpers

Equal contact counts do not guarantee straight-through wiring. Crossed pairs, reversed orientation, keyed housings, polarity and shield or drain positions are documented circuit by circuit.

Short power distribution jumpers

Connects power entry, fuse, relay, terminal point and local load over a short path. Conductor size, terminal rating, branch loading, voltage drop and protection coordination are reviewed together.

Service, test and replacement jumpers

Used for maintenance access, temporary test connection, module replacement or retrofit work. Clear labels, protected contacts and controlled compatibility help prevent an incorrect connection during service.

Material Recommendations

Conductor size and insulation

Wire size and strand construction are selected from circuit current, voltage drop, flexibility, terminal range and route. PVC, XLPE, silicone, fluoropolymer or another specified insulation is reviewed against temperature, chemicals, movement and applicable equipment documents.

Connector pitch, keying and housing material

A visually similar housing may have a different pitch, latch, polarization, cavity numbering or resin grade. Original part numbers, mating samples or verified drawings reduce the risk of a jumper that looks correct but cannot mate.

Terminals, contacts and crimp system

Contact plating, compatible wire range, seal range, strip length, crimp tooling and retention feature belong to one termination system. Ring, fork, blade, receptacle, ferrule and connector contacts are not selected by appearance alone.

Sleeves, labels and contact protection

Heat-shrink, braid, small conduit, terminal boots, cavity plugs and durable labels are added where the route or service method requires them. Exposed contacts and loose short leads need packaging and handling controls as well as electrical design.

Jumper Harness Solutions

Build from a pin-to-pin table

The approved table records cavity numbers, wire colours, conductor sizes, circuit names, shields, polarity and any crossed route. The harness drawing adds length datums, connector views, labels and inspection points.

Develop from samples without guessing

A reference jumper helps confirm dimensions, connector orientation and visible materials. Hidden pin routes, ratings, plating, internal splices and acceptance requirements are verified separately before the sample is copied.

Convert between different interfaces

When two modules use different connector families, the jumper is designed as a controlled adapter. Pin function, signal pairing, grounding, shield continuity, voltage and current compatibility are reviewed before defining the mechanical conversion.

Turn a hand-built link into repeatable production

A temporary field jumper can be converted into a drawing, BOM, controlled lengths, approved terminals, labels and an electrical test program. This creates a replaceable part instead of relying on technician memory.

Industries and Applications

Appliances and compact equipment

Short links between control boards, displays, motors, switches, sensors and local power points where enclosure space and assembly order are limited.

Industrial machines and production lines

Module jumpers, panel links and replacement adapters for sensors, relays, actuators, I/O points and machine subassemblies.

Laboratory, prototype and test systems

Defined patch leads and breakout jumpers for development benches, fixtures, measurement modules and engineering validation, with pin mapping that can be reproduced later.

Control cabinets and electrical panels

Pre-terminated jumpers between terminal blocks, breakers, relays, switches, door components and cabinet modules, prepared to the panel numbering and routing plan.

Instrumentation and measurement equipment

Low-level signal, excitation, communication and local power jumpers between instruments, acquisition boards, sensor interfaces and internal modules.

Maintenance, retrofit and replacement work

Adapter and service jumpers that bridge an available replacement module to an existing machine interface while keeping the approved electrical function traceable.

Jumper Harness Making Method

  1. Interface and requirement review

    Confirm both mating connector systems, circuit function, voltage, current, signal type, environment, quantity and required records before selecting materials.

  2. Pin map and dimensional definition

    Record cavity-to-cavity routing, connector views, insertion direction, length reference, exposed lead length, bend direction, labels and any straight, crossed or split circuit.

  3. Cutting, stripping and terminal preparation

    Prepare conductors to controlled cut and strip lengths, then crimp, solder or otherwise terminate with the approved contact system and tooling.

  4. Insertion, forming and identification

    Insert contacts into the correct cavities, set connector orientation, form the short lead without overstressing the wire exit, and apply circuit or part identification.

  5. Continuity, polarity and retention checks

    Verify the approved pin map, opens, shorts, polarity where applicable, terminal seating, retention, dimensions and visible workmanship before release.

  6. First article and controlled batch release

    The approved sample, drawing, BOM, test program and packaging method become the production reference. Later connector or pin changes are handled as recorded revisions.

Connection and Reliability Review

Wire exit, bend radius and pull direction

A short jumper can transfer installation force directly into a terminal. Length, service loop, bend direction, strain relief and pull path are reviewed so the contact is not used as a mechanical anchor.

Current, temperature rise and contact resistance

Conductor size alone does not define capacity. Terminal family, crimp quality, connector rating, contact resistance, bundling, ambient temperature and duty cycle all affect a short power jumper.

Environment and exposed contact protection

Dust, moisture, oil, cleaning agents and handling can affect unsealed connectors and exposed terminals. Seals, boots, plugs, sleeves or packaging are specified only for the actual exposure.

Test scope and compatibility boundary

Continuity confirms circuit routing but does not prove every signal, load or protocol is compatible. Functional, dielectric, insulation, temperature or endurance tests are added only when the project defines methods and acceptance limits.

What a Defined Jumper Improves

Faster installation with fewer wiring decisions

Predefined length, connector orientation, pin map and labels remove repeated cutting and point-to-point decisions during equipment assembly or service.

Traceable electrical function

A drawing and test map make crossed routes, polarity, shields and special circuits visible to engineering, production and maintenance teams instead of hiding them inside a short cable.

Repeatable replacement parts

Controlled components, dimensions, labels and inspection records allow a service jumper or retrofit adapter to be reordered without rebuilding it from memory.

A small assembly treated as an engineered part

Even a two-wire link receives interface, circuit, material and acceptance definitions appropriate to its function, reducing the risk created by an undocumented improvised connection.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a jumper harness always wired straight through?

No. A jumper can be straight-through, crossed, reversed, split or combined with shields and specified components. The approved pin-to-pin table is the production and test reference.

What information is needed for a quotation?

Provide both connector and mating part numbers, a pin table, circuit function, voltage and current, length datum, wire and colour requirements, environment, quantity and inspection requirements. Photos or samples can supplement missing drawings.

Can a jumper be developed from an existing sample?

Yes, but a sample cannot reveal every rating or hidden circuit reliably. Pin mapping, conductor specification, terminal system, plating, polarity and acceptance criteria still need confirmation before repeat production.

How should jumper length be measured?

The drawing should define the datum, such as connector face to connector face, wire exit to wire exit or cut length. It should also state whether the jumper is straight, naturally laid or formed during measurement.

Can a different connector replace the original one?

Only after reviewing mating fit, cavity numbering, terminal system, current, voltage, temperature, material, keying, locking and installation space. A similar shell shape is not evidence of compatibility.

Which checks are performed before shipment?

Finished jumpers receive continuity and appearance inspection. Pin mapping, polarity, terminal retention, dimensions, labels, insulation or other electrical and mechanical checks are added according to the approved project plan.
