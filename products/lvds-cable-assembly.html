LVDS Cable Assembly

Overview

LVDS cable assemblies carry high-speed differential data between processors, serializers, displays, cameras and control electronics. This category covers internal display looms, panel links, shielded twisted-pair assemblies and application-specific power/control branches—not one universal screen cable.

Design the Entire Signal Path

Pair geometry, impedance, shielding and display-system integration

A reliable LVDS assembly is a continuous high-speed path: transmitter launch, connector, cable pair, shield and receiver termination all matter. We define this path from the equipment interface outward, then design the cable construction and manufacturing controls around it.

    LVDS Cable Assembly Questions

    Is every display cable an LVDS cable?

    No. Displays may use LVDS, eDP, MIPI, FPD-Link, GMSL or proprietary parallel/serial interfaces. Connector appearance alone cannot identify the electrical interface.

    Can the cable be built from an old sample?

    A sample helps with connector, length and routing, but data rate, pin map, pair assignment, impedance, termination, shield grounding, power circuits and acceptance limits still require equipment data or engineering confirmation.

    Does 100-ohm cable guarantee a good LVDS link?

    No. Connector and board transitions, pair balance, length, loss, termination, stubs, grounding, crosstalk and the actual transmitter/receiver also affect the complete channel.

    Can power and backlight wires be included?

    Yes, when voltage, current, return path, sequencing, connector rating, separation and thermal conditions are defined. They are documented separately from the high-speed pairs.

    Why can a cable pass continuity but still show flicker?

    Continuity does not reveal impedance discontinuity, attenuation, skew, poor termination, intermittent contact, grounding noise or marginal timing. The agreed high-speed or functional test is needed.

    What information is needed for quotation?

    Provide both mating interfaces and drawings, equipment or chipset models, pin map, pair/lane count, data rate, target impedance, cable and branch lengths, auxiliary power/control circuits, shield/ground plan, movement and environment, quantity, labels and validation requirements.

    Configuration & Project Review

    01 Display and board identity

    Confirm the exact display and controller connector drawings; display size does not specify cable length.

    02 Pin map and power pins

    Define lane count, pair polarity, grounds, panel power and control pins from both device drawings.

    03 Pair and impedance plan

    Confirm the actual link architecture first. Differential-pair, 90-ohm, 100-ohm and video-coaxial constructions are distinct configurations.

    04 FFC or discrete cable

    Select FFC, micro-coax or twisted-pair construction from the interface and routing requirements.

    05 Fold and latch clearance

    Define permitted folds, contact orientation, latch access and strain relief near the board.

    06 Panel validation

    Test startup, image stability and interference with the intended panel and controller pair.

    Send the part number, drawing or sample to confirm the replacement configuration and sourcing plan.
