Braided Protection Harness

Overview

Braided protection harnesses use textile, expandable, metal or other specified braiding to protect or organize a cable bundle. Protective braid and conductive shielding braid serve different functions and are not interchangeable.

Product Introduction

Abrasion protection, bundle control and defined electrical shielding

A braided protection harness can use PET, nylon, aramid, glass fibre, tinned copper, stainless steel or another specified braid, but these materials do not perform the same job. Expandable textile braid mainly organizes and protects the bundle. Conductive braid can form part of an EMI shielding system only when coverage, termination and grounding are designed. The finished harness therefore depends on bundle diameter, expansion range, abrasion, temperature, chemicals, movement, branch geometry and end treatment.

Braided Protection Configurations

Expandable PET abrasion braid

Lightweight expandable braid organizes irregular bundles and provides general abrasion resistance. The nominal size must cover both the minimum and maximum bundle diameter without excessive looseness or tension.

Flexible textile and nylon braid

Soft textile or nylon systems are used where repeated contact, flexibility, noise reduction or a particular surface feel matters. Fibre, weave and edge treatment are selected from the actual duty cycle.

High-temperature glass fibre or aramid braid

Heat-resistant braid protects bundles near heaters, engines or hot equipment surfaces. Temperature exposure, flexing, coating and fibre shedding must be reviewed rather than relying on a generic high-temperature label.

Tinned-copper EMI shielding braid

Conductive braid can provide shielding when material, optical coverage, transfer path, shield termination and chassis or circuit grounding are defined as one system.

Stainless-steel and heavy abrasion braid

Metal or reinforced braid is considered for severe abrasion, mechanical protection or specialized environments. Weight, bend stiffness, cut edges, corrosion pairing and termination influence suitability.

Hybrid braid, conduit and sealed transitions

One harness may combine braid for flexible runs, conduit for exposed zones and sealed boots or backshells at interfaces. Transition points are designed so protection does not end at the most vulnerable location.

Braid and Material Recommendations

Braid fibre, wire and weave construction

Material chemistry, filament or wire diameter, weave density, expansion ratio, colour and coating affect abrasion, flexibility, temperature and compatibility with oils or cleaning agents.

Underlying wire, cable and connector system

Braiding does not correct an unsuitable conductor, insulation, cable jacket or connector. The protected bundle is first defined electrically and mechanically, then the outer braid is selected around it.

Shield termination and grounding hardware

Crimp ferrules, shield rings, backshells, drain wires, grounding leads and conductive tapes are selected for the required termination geometry and current or frequency path.

End fixation, labels and cut-edge treatment

Heat-shrink, boots, clamps, thread, tape or molded strain relief can secure braid ends. Hot cutting, sealed edges or covered transitions prevent fraying according to the material and service needs.

Braided Harness Solutions

Select braid from the real protection goal

The project first separates abrasion, bundle management, appearance, heat exposure and electrical shielding. Material and construction are then chosen for the required functions instead of treating every braid as equivalent.

Define shielding coverage and termination

For EMI projects, braid coverage, overlap, branch continuity, connector backshell, drain path and grounding location are recorded. Shielding performance requires a defined test method and complete installed configuration.

Manage branches and moving transitions

Braid size, branch breakout, overlap, end fixation and bend path are coordinated so the covering follows movement without bunching, opening or loading the connector exit.

Add protection to an existing harness

A representative sample is measured at connectors, branches and maximum bundle points. Installation access, removable components and required disassembly determine whether braid can be added without redesigning the loom.

Industries and Applications

Robotics and repeated-motion equipment

Flexible protective braid organizes cables around moving arms and tooling while branch, bend and attachment points remain matched to the motion path.

Machine tools and industrial equipment

Abrasion, chips, oil and service contact drive braid, conduit and transition choices around cabinets, spindles, sensors and auxiliary systems.

Heating and high-temperature zones

Glass fibre, aramid or specified high-temperature braid protects cable surfaces near heaters, ovens and thermal equipment when the full temperature profile is known.

Instrumentation and noise-sensitive systems

Conductive braid and controlled terminations can support shielding for measurement, sensor and communication circuits where the grounding scheme is defined.

Outdoor, marine and process equipment

Moisture, salt, chemicals and sunlight require braid material and underlying cable jackets that are compatible with the complete exposure, not just an attractive outer weave.

Commercial equipment and visible harnesses

Neat abrasion-resistant braid supports organized, serviceable visible cable runs in kiosks, appliances, packaging equipment and production fixtures.

Braided Harness Making Method

  1. Environment and function review

    Confirm abrasion, heat, chemicals, movement, shielding, appearance, bundle range, branches, quantities and required validation before material selection.

  2. Representative bundle measurement

    Measure minimum, nominal and maximum bundle diameters at straight sections, branches, splices and connector exits, including any tape or underlying conduit.

  3. Braid preparation and application

    Cut or braid to controlled lengths, manage fray, apply over the formed harness and maintain the specified overlap, tension and branch transitions.

  4. Termination and end fixation

    Secure ends with approved heat-shrink, clamps, backshells, ferrules, thread, tape or molding without creating sharp edges or blocking connector locks.

  5. Coverage, flexibility and electrical inspection

    Check braid coverage, branch openings, end fixation, flexibility, connector operation and appearance. Conductive shielding tests are added only to the approved method.

  6. First article and controlled release

    The approved sample records braid material, size, coverage, cut method, transition geometry, termination and inspection points for later production.

Protection and Shielding Review

Flexibility, bunching and fatigue

A braid that is too tight can restrict movement, while an oversized braid can bunch or rub. Expansion, bend radius, attachment spacing and motion cycle are reviewed together.

Abrasion, chemicals and contamination

Fibre and coating resistance are checked against edges, chips, oil, coolant, cleaners, dust and the actual contact surface. A cosmetic braid may not survive industrial exposure.

Shield continuity and grounding

Conductive braid performance depends on coverage, low-impedance termination, branch continuity, connector backshell and grounding topology. Visual metal coverage alone is not an EMI result.

Validation scope and claims

Abrasion, temperature, flammability, shielding, ingress or endurance claims require defined specimens, methods and acceptance limits. Category names do not replace project validation.

What Defined Braiding Improves

Cleaner and more serviceable bundles

Defined braid size, branch openings and removable end treatments organize cables without permanently hiding the interfaces technicians need to reach.

Protection matched to the actual duty

Materials are selected for abrasion, heat, chemicals, motion or shielding as separate functions, reducing unnecessary weight and unsupported claims.

Repeatable branch and termination quality

Controlled overlap, transitions, end fixation and inspection points reproduce the approved covering across later batches.

A complete protected harness assembly

The underlying electrical harness, outer protection, connector transitions, labels and grounding features are reviewed and delivered as one assembly.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does every braided harness provide EMI shielding?

No. PET, nylon and other textile braids mainly provide organization and mechanical protection. EMI shielding requires conductive material, defined coverage, termination, grounding and an agreed validation method.

What information is needed for a quotation?

State the protection goal, braid material if mandatory, minimum and maximum bundle diameters, branches, temperature, chemicals, movement, termination, colour, quantity and required tests.

Can braid be added to an existing harness?

Often yes, when connector dimensions, removable parts, branch geometry and installation access allow the selected braid to pass and terminate correctly. A representative sample should be reviewed.

How is braid size selected?

Selection uses the complete diameter range, expansion ratio, required coverage, bend and branch geometry. Nominal diameter alone is not enough because excessive expansion changes coverage and stiffness.

How are braid ends prevented from fraying?

Depending on material and service needs, ends may be hot-cut, sealed, folded, covered with heat-shrink, tied, clamped, terminated in a backshell or incorporated into molding.

Which inspections are performed before shipment?

Finished assemblies receive continuity and appearance inspection plus checks of coverage, branch transitions, end fixation, flexibility and connector operation. Shielding or environmental tests follow the approved plan.

Configuration & Project Review

01 Mechanical protection or shielding

Specify whether the sleeve provides abrasion protection, bundle organisation or electrical shielding. Conductive braid requires a separate end-termination and grounding definition.

02 Maximum bundle and connector envelope

Measure the largest bundle, splice and connector that the sleeve must pass over. Check expansion and recovery on a sample; nominal braid size alone does not define the installation fit.

03 Split or closed construction

Choose closed sleeving for assembly before termination or a split/reclosable sleeve for later access. Define overlap at bends and branch exits to avoid gaps after routing.

04 Cut edges and branch transitions

Match cutting and edge restraint to the sleeve material. Confirm end fixation, local heat-shrink or binding and the transition at every breakout on the first article.

05 Environment and rubbing points

Identify recurring contact with brackets, fluids, heat and moving equipment. Review sleeve material and local reinforcement at those points instead of applying one protection choice everywhere.

06 Appearance and repeatable coverage

Define the exposed length, braid coverage, fastening locations and branch appearance on the sample. Use these dimensions as production inspection points rather than a general appearance request.
