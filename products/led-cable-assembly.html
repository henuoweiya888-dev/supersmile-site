LED Cable Assembly

Overview

LED cable assemblies connect drivers, controllers, dimmers, switches, distribution points, light engines, strips and luminaires. The category includes simple two-wire power leads, multi-channel RGB or RGBW harnesses, addressable-light power-and-data assemblies, sealed outdoor branches, automotive relay harnesses and equipment-internal lighting looms.

Wire the Light as a Complete System

Power, control, sealing and repeatable light-system wiring

An LED harness can carry power only, power plus several color channels, or power together with timing-sensitive control data. We define what each conductor does, what reaches the final light after cable loss, and how every joint survives its real installation before selecting wire and connectors.

    LED Cable Assembly Questions

    Can LED wire size be selected from wattage alone?

    No. Supply voltage, current per channel, run length, complete return path, allowable drop, connector losses, ambient and local temperature, bundling and duty cycle all affect selection.

    Can one four-pin connector be used for every RGB system?

    No. Systems can differ in voltage, common-anode or common-cathode arrangement, channel order, current, connector keying and control method. The exact pin map and mating device must be confirmed.

    Does an IP67 connector make the whole LED harness IP67?

    No. The complete assembly boundary includes cable entry, wire seals, mating interface, branch transitions, splices, end caps and enclosure entries. The agreed test configuration must cover the finished assembly.

    Can you build a harness from an LED controller and lamp samples?

    Samples help confirm fit and visible pin locations, but voltage, channel definition, current, control behavior, environmental rating and acceptance limits still require documentation or engineering confirmation.

    Why do LED lights become dimmer at the end of a run?

    Cable, connector and joint resistance create voltage drop, and daisy-chained current makes early sections carry more load. Conductor size, branch topology, feed position and power-injection strategy should be reviewed.

    What information is needed for quotation?

    Provide LED and driver/controller models, voltage and current, pin or channel map, dimming/data protocol, both interfaces, cable and branch lengths, allowable drop, environment and IP target, movement, mounting, quantity, labels and validation requirements.
