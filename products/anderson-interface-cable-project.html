POWERPOLE / SB / CONTACT / DUTY

One interface name, several power paths

Start with the equipment circuit, not a connector silhouette. The load, service disconnect, mating half and assembly access decide whether a modular Powerpole set, a keyed SB pair or a different approved interface belongs on the drawing.

01

Interface families and keying

MODULAR SYSTEM

Powerpole: build the pole arrangement deliberately

Anderson Powerpole housings are modular and can be stacked into the pole arrangement a project needs. Their flat-wiping contacts and available contact options make them useful for disconnectable power circuits, but the assembled color order, housing orientation and contact part number are part of the electrical definition. A red-and-black pair in a photograph shows one arrangement, not a universal polarity rule. For battery jumpers, equipment power leads or test fixtures, document both mating views and the completed block orientation so the assembler cannot mirror a correct-looking pair. Confirm the exact Powerpole size and contact against the manufacturer's data before choosing conductor and crimp tooling.

Map polarity from mating views

Color and left-right position are not a substitute for a signed circuit drawing.

Lock the contact choice

Housing and contact are a matched selection, not independent substitutions.

TWO-POLE SYSTEM

SB: color and geometry help prevent the wrong mate

The Anderson SB range uses genderless two-pole housings for battery and DC power distribution. The manufacturer offers different body sizes and color-keyed configurations; identical-looking two-pole leads therefore do not automatically mate or carry the same contact system. Color is a selection and identification aid, not a license to force two parts together. Before ordering a charger or equipment replacement lead, confirm the SB series, housing color, mechanical key, contact and approved mating half from actual part references. If auxiliary signaling or battery monitoring is required, identify those circuits separately rather than implying every SB power pair contains them.

Verify the exact mating half

The series label and a photograph alone do not establish interchangeability.

Separate power and signal

Auxiliary contacts, if specified, need their own pin and test plan.

02

Current path and termination

CONDUCTOR & CONTACT

Size the entire current path, not the plastic shell

A high-current connector is only one element of a current path. The conductor cross-section, copper construction, cable length, voltage drop, contact plating, mating resistance, duty cycle and installed temperature must be considered together. Select the contact variant intended for the approved wire size and stripping range; an oversized barrel on a smaller conductor can make a neat-looking but unreliable crimp. For mobile equipment, forklifts, charging stations or battery racks, also account for flexing, abrasion, routing and the strain transferred to the connector. A generic current figure without the exact product data and thermal conditions should not be copied into a cable drawing.

Check drop and temperature

Continuous and intermittent loads may need different assumptions.

Use the approved barrel

Confirm contact-to-wire compatibility before any tooling trial.

TERMINATION

Crimp, insert and retain as separate checks

A contact can be crimped correctly yet seated incompletely, or seated well with a poor wire crimp. Control cut and strip length, conductor position, specified die and tool, crimp dimensions, insulation clearance and contact insertion. The close-up photo shows terminal-and-wire preparation, not an approved Super Smile process record. Once the contact is in the housing, verify retention and the correct pole position, then add cable support so equipment motion cannot load the contact. The first-article checklist should distinguish visual assessment, dimensional or pull checks where specified, and electrical continuity; none by itself proves the assembly's performance at its full rated duty.

Tooling is part of the BOM

The contact, wire and die combination must match the approved method.

Retain the cable, not just the pin

Route and support the lead so vibration or handling does not pull on the contact.

03

Equipment context and first article

EQUIPMENT CONTEXT

Battery equipment changes the cable specification

Material-handling equipment is one application context for disconnectable battery power; the pictured forklift does not show or certify an Anderson connector. An AGV, floor machine, charger, UPS battery cabinet or test stand may put the mating point in a very different place. Check whether operators unplug daily, whether the lead moves with the equipment, how it is protected against impact or abrasion, and how the charger or battery-management system identifies the connection. Only then set cable length, bend relief, labeling and any specified auxiliary circuits. Do not infer vehicle compatibility from a connector family name.

Locate the service point

Access and unplug frequency affect routing and support.

Review the full system

A cable does not validate a charger or battery-management strategy.

FIRST ARTICLE

Release against a drawing and a mating sample

A sample cable is useful, but it may hide a substituted contact, a reversed pole pair or a field repair. For a repeatable Anderson interface assembly, freeze the manufacturer part references, mating view, circuit and polarity table, cut length, wire and contact BOM, crimp method, protective parts and inspection points before releasing volume production. Review a first article on the actual approved mating half where available; record contact seating, keying, labels, cable reach and agreed electrical checks. The workshop photograph is general illustrative context, not a record of this product being produced or tested by Super Smile. Any rated-load, temperature-rise or system-level claim needs its own defined test and evidence.

Record the approved configuration

Keep the exact housing, contact and mating revisions traceable.

Define acceptance separately

Continuity is not a temperature-rise or equipment-safety test.

Anderson Interface Questions

Are all Anderson-style plugs interchangeable?

No. Powerpole and SB are different families, and SB size, color key, housing and contact versions must be matched. Confirm complete part numbers and the approved mating half rather than relying on shape.

Does the connector series determine cable current rating?

No. The contact option, wire cross-section, installation temperature, duty cycle, cable length and mating condition all affect the assembly. Use the exact manufacturer data and project thermal criteria.

Can a compatible interface be specified without an exact part number?

A photograph or used sample can start the review, but it cannot close it. Confirm the specified series, housing key, contact, mating view and sourcing approval before the first article. Compatibility is project-specific and does not imply brand affiliation or certification.

How is polarity controlled on a modular Powerpole cable?

Use a signed circuit and mating-view drawing, mark the assembled housing orientation, then check wire identity and pole position on the approved mating sample during first-article inspection.

What should we send for an Anderson interface cable quotation?

Send the manufacturer, full series and part references, mating-side image, circuit/polarity drawing, continuous and peak load, voltage, wire and length requirements, other-end connector, equipment duty, quantity and requested inspection or validation.

Configuration & Project Review

01 Interface & Application

Powerpole modular housings and two-pole SB housings are different connection systems. A family name alone does not establish mating fit, current rating or polarity. Contact part number, cable cross-section, temperature rise, assembly method and the manufacturer's series-specific documentation all matter.

02 Project Inputs

Supply the manufacturer and complete connector series or part number, mating-side photograph and contact view, circuit and polarity drawing, continuous and peak load, voltage, wire size and cable length, opposite-end interface, installation environment, quantity and acceptance criteria. Identify auxiliary contacts or battery-monitoring circuits separately.

03 Release Requirements

The approved inputs become a controlled connector-and-contact BOM, cable drawing, polarity map, assembly instructions and first-article checklist. The inspection scope can cover wire identity, crimp geometry, contact retention, housing keying, cable length and agreed electrical checks. Continuity alone cannot prove rated-load temperature, charging-system compatibility or equipment safety.

04 Mating & Termination

Identify the mating part number and termination method before confirming the assembly drawing.

05 Sample Review

Compare the sample with the approved pin map, dimensions and material list.

06 Build Records

Retain the approved part list and inspection results for repeat production.

Send the part number, drawing or sample to confirm the replacement configuration and sourcing plan.
