Yazaki Interface Harness Project

Overview

Sensor leads, vehicle branches, equipment adapters and removable harnesses built around customer-specified Yazaki connectors. We verify the exact housing, terminal, wire seal, secondary lock and mating half before defining pins, cable and routing; a family name is not a complete configuration.

HOUSING / TERMINAL / SEAL / ROUTE

Start with the exact interface, not a similar silhouette

A vehicle harness crosses engine bay, body, cabin and serviceable modules. The connector does more than conduct: it locates, retains and protects an interface. Mark fixed and removable halves and separate signal and power circuits; then verify the family and every matched part in the Yazaki catalog. These documentary photos illustrate connection and installation contexts, not a claim that pictured parts are Yazaki originals or our shipments.

01

Interface families and matched parts

HOUSING & KEYING

YESC and YMT identify families, not a buildable harness

Yazaki's public catalog lists YESC, YMT 1.2, 0.64 (025), 1.5 (060) and 2.3 (090) families across sealed, unsealed and mixed-cavity designs. Equal cavity count does not make terminal cavities, color keys, latches, secondary locks or mating halves interchangeable. Record full housing references and suffixes, the mating-face orientation of both halves, then map every circuit to a pin. The photo shows real in-vehicle multipole connectors, not an identified Yazaki model.

Read the mating face

Specify male/female half, viewing direction and cavity numbering.

Include locks in the BOM

Secondary locks, CPA and cavity plugs cannot be left to shop-floor guesswork.

SEALED INTERFACE

A sealed family needs wire seals, cavity plugs and correct mating

Families such as YMT 1.2 Waterproof address specified sealed applications, but 'waterproof' is not a property of the plastic shell alone. Each wire seal must match insulation diameter and terminal design; unused cavities need the specified plugs; interface seal, latch and mate must be complete. The photo shows a different vehicle's sensor connection and cable protection, illustrating splash, debris and strain paths—not any Yazaki or IP rating. Define water, mud, oil, wash and temperature duty, then agree the applicable parts and tests.

Measure insulation OD

Conductor area does not determine seal fit.

Check unused cavities and mate

Missing plugs or incomplete mating can defeat the intended protection.

02

Sealing, termination and wire

CONTACT TERMINATION

Review terminal, tooling, wire and retention as a set

Open-barrel and other terminal designs differ in crimp window, conductor range, insulation support and tooling. Use the exact terminal reference to set strip length, crimp height or manufacturer-defined process measures, and document visual, retention and pull-check criteria. The pictured generic hand tools are not Yazaki-approved tooling and imply no terminal fit. Passing continuity only shows a circuit is connected at test time; it does not prove crimp geometry, terminal seating or long-term vibration performance.

Do not transfer tooling by appearance

Terminal reference and crimp specification precede die selection.

Separate the checks

Visual, retention, pull and electrical checks answer different questions.

POWER & SIGNAL

One assembly can contain several terminal sizes and branches

Sensors, actuators, body controls and auxiliary feeds do not share one wire gauge. Yazaki's catalog separates families by contact size and seal design, while hybrid connectors may carry different circuits in one housing. Select conductors for current, voltage drop, temperature, bundling and intermittent duty, then confirm the terminal's conductor and insulation ranges. Preserve specified twist, shielding and ground path for data or noise-sensitive lines. The photo is a removed older-vehicle cluster harness illustrating branches and mixed connectors, not evidence of Yazaki parts.

Do not infer current from shape

Review contact, wire and environment together.

Preserve signal construction

Twist and shield termination follow the controller design.

03

Vehicle routing and release

VEHICLE ROUTE

Fixing points, pass-throughs and service position set dimensions

Engine bay, underbody and cabin differ in heat, fluids, abrasion and moving parts. The drawing needs pass-through location, sleeve or grommet, clips, bend radius, branch datums, service allowance and no-contact zones. Too short can load terminals; too long can reach heat or pinch points. The photo shows another vehicle's firewall pass-through, not a validated seal for this project. A used sample still needs installed-position photos and fixing-point measurements; flat-bench length alone is insufficient.

Dimension from datums

Clips, mating faces and branch points need repeatable datums.

Leave a service path

The connector must be removable, not merely mateable.

FIRST ARTICLE & TEST

Release pin map, locking and test results separately

Compare both housing references, terminals and seals, pin map, wire colors and labels to the approved BOM on the first article. Then confirm fully seated terminals, closed secondary locks, unused-cavity plugs, branch dimensions and mating orientation; record continuity, shorts and any agreed electrical checks against explicit limits. The photo illustrates probing a generic machine, not a test result for this harness. Ingress, vibration or vehicle-regulatory compliance needs separately defined standards, samples and acceptance limits—not an inference from a photo or a continuity pass.

Check both ends together

A pin map must state the viewing direction to avoid mirroring.

Claim only what was verified

Project tests and vehicle certification have separate boundaries.

Yazaki interface harness questions

Is 'Yazaki 2.3' enough to build the harness?

No. Specify the exact family and housing suffix, cavity count, keying, terminals, seals or plugs, mating half, pin map and duty.

Does a sealed housing make the whole harness waterproof?

No. Wire-seal fit, cavity plugs, interface seal, mating condition and installation all matter; verify against exact parts and agreed tests.

Can an existing sample be copied?

A sample helps with shape, branches and exit direction; hidden pin functions, substitute parts, load and environment still require customer confirmation.

Are CPA, TPA and a secondary lock the same thing?

No. Terminal-position, connector-position and secondary locking features serve different purposes in different families; use the exact drawing and assembly guide.

Are the photographed connectors genuine Yazaki parts?

The documentary photos illustrate connection structures and vehicle contexts; they do not prove origin, authorization or delivery. Purchase and substitution follow customer-approved part references.

Configuration & Project Review

01 Interface & Application

Yazaki's automotive connector catalog spans sealed and unsealed, wire-to-wire and device-side structures. Names such as YESC, YMT 1.2, 0.64 (025), 1.5 (060) and 2.3 (090) help locate a family, but cavity count, keying, terminal size and sealing must still be resolved by part number and drawing.

02 Project Inputs

For quotation, provide housing and mating part numbers, mating-face pin views, terminals and seals, circuit map, conductors, environment and temperature, fixing points, branch dimensions, quantity and acceptance criteria. Photos or an old sample aid identification but do not authorize unseen electrical functions or substitute parts.

03 Release Requirements

Approved inputs become a controlled BOM, pin map, assembly instructions and first-article checkpoints. Check crimps, terminal retention, seal position, secondary lock, branch dimensions, continuity and mating. Ingress, vibration, dielectric or vehicle-level suitability requires exact component data and separately agreed project validation.

04 Mating & Termination

Identify the mating part number and termination method before confirming the assembly drawing.

05 Sample Review

Compare the sample with the approved pin map, dimensions and material list.

06 Build Records

Retain the approved part list and inspection results for repeat production.

Send the part number, drawing or sample to confirm the replacement configuration and sourcing plan.
