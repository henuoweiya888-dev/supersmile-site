Product Introduction

One controlled supply scope from drawing to packed assembly

A turnkey harness is defined by responsibility, not by appearance. The supply scope may include engineering review, source-controlled wires and connectors, customer-supplied parts, terminals, seals, fuses, relays, clips, labels, protective coverings, fixtures, electrical tests, records and unit packaging. The purpose is to deliver an approved assembly that can be installed or issued to production without the customer coordinating many separate component and process suppliers.

Turnkey Supply Configurations

Drawing-controlled finished harnesses

Complete looms are produced to approved drawings, BOMs, work instructions and inspection plans, with every connector, branch, label and protective feature included in the released configuration.

Panel and equipment wiring kits

Multiple related harnesses, jumpers, grounding leads and accessories are grouped by machine, cabinet, station or assembly step to simplify line-side issue and installation.

Harnesses with connectors, seals and protection

The delivered assembly includes mating-specific contacts, seals, locks, cavity plugs, conduit, braid, grommets and strain relief rather than leaving critical finishing work to the customer.

Fuse, relay and distribution subassemblies

Fuse holders, relays, sockets, bus links and labeled branches can be assembled and electrically checked as one controlled module when the project defines the ratings and acceptance plan.

Customer-supplied and mixed-source assemblies

Customer-supplied devices can be combined with sourced wires, connectors and accessories under documented receiving, ownership, shortage and replacement rules.

Installation-ready labeled and packed sets

Harnesses are labeled, protected, coiled or formed, counted and packed to the agreed unit so production receives the correct assembly in the required sequence.

Material and Source Recommendations

Wire, cable and conductor source control

Manufacturer, construction, gauge, insulation, colour, temperature range and required documents are fixed in the BOM or approved source list. Equivalent wording alone does not authorize a substitution.

Connector and terminal systems

Housings, contacts, seals, locks, plating and wire ranges are controlled as complete interface systems. Mating part data is retained so purchasing changes do not silently break compatibility.

Protection, fastening and installation accessories

Conduit, braid, tape, heat-shrink, boots, clips, ties, grommets, brackets and hardware are selected from the real route, environment and assembly method, then included in the controlled supply scope.

Labels, records and packaging materials

Part labels, circuit identification, inspection records, protective caps, bags, trays and cartons are specified according to traceability, transport and line-side handling needs.

Turnkey Project Solutions

Turn incomplete data into a controlled package

Drawings, pin tables, samples, equipment photos and component data are reviewed for gaps. Assumptions and open points are recorded before the drawing, BOM and inspection plan are released.

Convert a legacy sample into repeat production

An existing assembly is measured and electrically mapped, while hidden ratings, unavailable parts and undocumented processes are identified for customer review before a repeatable specification is frozen.

Manage difficult parts and substitutions

Long-lead or unavailable items are flagged early. Alternatives are compared by mating, rating, material, tooling, approval documents, installation impact and customer authorization before release.

Deliver by kit, station or production sequence

Packing units, quantities, labels and protective methods are aligned with the customer production sequence so correct harnesses and accessories reach the correct station together.

Turnkey Harness Delivery Method

  1. Supply boundary and requirement review

    Define what is included, customer-supplied parts, approved manufacturers, expected records, quantity, packaging unit and revision authority.

  2. BOM, source and availability confirmation

    Verify each material, source, lead time, minimum quantity, substitution rule and incoming requirement before committing the production schedule.

  3. First-article assembly and fit review

    Build the representative assembly, confirm routing, connector direction, branch geometry, accessories, labels and installation against the agreed equipment or fixture.

  4. Controlled production and in-process checks

    Use released work documents, fixtures, calibrated or maintained tools and defined in-process points for cutting, termination, assembly and protection.

  5. Electrical and workmanship inspection

    Check circuit mapping, continuity, polarity, terminal retention, dimensions, labels and appearance, with additional tests performed only to the approved project plan.

  6. Kit verification, records and shipment release

    Confirm item count, accessory completeness, inspection status, protective packing, labels and required records before each production unit is released.

Configuration and Quality Control

Drawing, BOM and test alignment

The released drawing, BOM, work instruction and test program must describe the same revision. A change in one document cannot be allowed to create an undocumented mixed configuration.

Substitution and deviation control

Material deviations record the reason, affected quantity, technical comparison, approval and expiry. Temporary acceptance does not automatically change the standard BOM.

Fixture and workmanship repeatability

Branch boards, applicators, insertion aids and inspection fixtures are maintained so later batches reproduce dimensions, terminations and assembly sequence rather than only matching appearance.

Inspection records and compliance boundary

Records cover the agreed harness checks. Complete equipment certification, environmental qualification or regulatory claims require separately defined responsibility, samples and responsible laboratories.

What Turnkey Supply Improves

Engineering, sourcing, assembly, inspection and packaging are coordinated within one approved supply scope instead of being split among disconnected vendors.

Clear configuration ownership

Approved sources, customer-supplied parts, revisions, substitutions and records have named ownership, reducing schedule and compatibility disputes.

Assemblies ready for installation and service

Correct labels, accessories, protection and packing arrive together, reducing line-side preparation and missing-component interruptions.

Repeat orders reproduce the approved result

A frozen package of drawing, BOM, process, fixture, test and packing data carries the approved first article into later production and controlled revisions.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does turnkey supply include?

The exact scope is project-specific. It can include engineering review, sourcing, customer-supplied parts, harness assembly, accessories, labels, inspection, records, kitting and packaging. The quotation and approved documents define the boundary.

Can you work with customer-supplied parts?

Yes, when receiving condition, quantities, ownership, shortage handling, unused return, traceability and responsibility for part suitability are agreed before production.

How are substitutions handled?

Alternatives are compared technically and commercially, then released only through the agreed customer approval route. A temporary deviation is recorded separately from the standard BOM.

Can an existing sample become a turnkey product?

Yes, after dimensions, circuits, materials, hidden joints, acceptance criteria, unavailable components and packing requirements are converted into controlled documents and approved.

Which records can accompany shipment?

Available records depend on the project and may include inspection status, electrical test output, material or lot traceability and packing lists. Required formats must be agreed before quotation or release.

Does turnkey supply mean the complete equipment is certified?

No. Turnkey describes the agreed supply responsibility. Complete equipment certification and regulatory compliance remain separate activities unless methods, samples, laboratories and responsibility are explicitly included.
