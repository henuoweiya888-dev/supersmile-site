TE / AMP Compatible Assemblies

Overview

Custom connector leads and harness assemblies defined around specified TE / AMP interfaces, terminal systems, wire, sealing and installation requirements.

Ask About This Product TypeBack to Products

Product Introduction

This category covers sealed automotive pigtails, multiway equipment looms, panel connections, appliance power leads, industrial control wiring and project-specific high-voltage assemblies. Super Smile develops the cable assembly around the interface specified for your equipment; it is not one universal “AMP plug”. TE Connectivity, AMP and the product-family names on this page identify the requested interface, not an affiliation or authorization. A quotation should distinguish genuine specified components from any separately proposed alternative. Substitution requires explicit approval of the mating geometry, electrical duty and verification plan before production.

01

What Makes an Assembly Compatible?

Start with the complete housing and mating-half part numbers, including suffixes. Then record the contact system, position count, cavity view, polarization, latch and terminal retention. A shell that slides over another shell is not proof of a sound electrical interface. Contact engagement, retention, seal compression and service access all need to work together. For repair pigtails and adapter leads, identify each end independently and keep the connection table with the drawing. This turns a visual match into a traceable specification. When the original part is unknown, provide sharp front, rear and side photographs with dimensions rather than a brand name alone.

02

SUPERSEAL 1.5: Sealing Is a System

AMP SUPERSEAL 1.5 includes one- to six-position inline configurations for compact sealed wiring. The 1.5 designation identifies the contact family; it is not the cable cross-section or a universal cavity pitch. A sealed assembly combines the correct housing, contacts, wire seals, secondary locks and any required cavity plugs. Match each seal to the specified insulation diameter, not just the copper size. The family’s stated IP67 performance relates to the defined assembly and test conditions; an open, damaged or incorrectly populated connector does not inherit it. For a waterproof harness enquiry, describe splash direction, immersion exposure, chemicals and the condition in which protection is required.

03

AMPSEAL and AMPSEAL 16 Are Different

AMPSEAL multiway systems and AMPSEAL 16 should not be treated as one interchangeable connector. AMPSEAL 16 provides sealed wire-to-wire and wire-to-device options, including panel installation. Its contact selection, terminal position assurance and connector position assurance must follow the specific configuration. Do not carry a temperature rating or permitted wire range from one variant to another. In a machine or off-highway harness, check whether a bulkhead flange, backshell, strain relief or service loop is required. The mating drawing should control mounting clearance as well as the connector itself, particularly where gloves, tools or nearby hoses restrict access.

04

MATE-N-LOK: Family Before Appearance

Universal MATE-N-LOK, Mini-Universal MATE-N-LOK and Micro MATE-N-LOK belong to distinct systems. Similar white housings or a matching position count are not enough to select a mate. Check the family, cavity shape, contact gender and the specified wire range. As one concrete example, TE 926869-1 is a Universal MATE-N-LOK receptacle contact with its own conductor range, material and application documents; it is not a generic terminal for every MATE-N-LOK housing. Appliance power leads and cabinet looms also need attention to loaded circuits, routing and any required panel retention. Use the exact terminal drawing and application specification when choosing crimp tooling.

05

FASTON: Width Is Only One Dimension

FASTON quick-disconnect systems are commonly described by tab width, but tab thickness, receptacle design, material and plating are also part of the connection. Straight and flag receptacles solve different access and routing problems. A generic “6.3 mm spade” description does not identify the full interface or establish a TE origin. Record the tab drawing, wire specification, insulation support and required housing or sleeve. For appliance, relay and grounding leads, evaluate the complete assembled connection under its actual electrical and thermal duty. A loose fit should not be corrected by squeezing a receptacle by hand; it calls for the correct mating components and termination process.

06

Dynamic Series: Control Wiring by Function

Industrial control harnesses can combine power, signal and protective connections, but their interfaces should remain clearly differentiated. In TE Dynamic Series, the selected series and keying determine the mating system. Certain D3000 configurations provide first-mate/last-break ground contacts; this is a configuration-specific feature, not a property of every Dynamic connector. Begin the harness drawing with circuit function, load and maintenance access. Separate signal identification from conductor colour, and define the point from which branch lengths are measured. A cabinet lead is easier to install and service when labels, mating references and routing instructions agree with the equipment drawing.

07

AMP+ HVA: A Separate High-Voltage Review

AMP+ HVA 280 is a high-voltage interface family with shielding and high-voltage interlock options. The interlock circuit is a system function; it should not be bridged or omitted to make a cable appear operational. Connector configuration, validated cable, shield termination, keying and mating device must be reviewed together. An orange housing alone does not define voltage capability or prove that an assembly is safe. Before quotation, establish the insulation coordination, mechanical route, approved components and required verification with the equipment owner. High-voltage production or testing requires suitable procedures and competent personnel; inclusion in this category is an invitation to engineering review, not a blanket qualification claim.

08

Materials: Contact, Wire and Insulation Together

A connector BOM extends beyond the visible housing. It can include conductor and insulation variants, contact plating, cable seals, cavity plugs, backshells, sleeving and identification. Select a terminal for both the conductor range and the insulation support it was designed to crimp. Preserve the contact material and plating specified for the mating pair; an attractive finish is not an electrical specification. For a proposed alternative, compare the relevant drawings and application documents, then define the checks needed for approval. The harness drawing should also state strip length, branch dimensions, protection transitions and any controlled material substitutions so that a repeat order remains the same engineering configuration.

09

Manufacturing and Inspection

The working sequence is part identification, BOM and drawing review, tooling confirmation, wire preparation, termination, contact insertion, secondary-lock assembly and final verification. Crimp setup follows the terminal application specification rather than a general plier setting. First-article review should reconcile cavity numbering, contact seating, seals, dimensions and the actual test map. Continuity can reveal open or incorrect circuits, but does not by itself demonstrate sealing, retention or environmental durability. Additional mechanical, electrical or environmental checks need defined acceptance criteria and an agreed scope. Keep approved samples and controlled records so that changes in materials or tooling are reviewed instead of silently entering later batches.

10

Automotive Applications: Exposure Changes the Brief

Engine-bay connections, chassis sensors, exterior lighting and cabin equipment face different combinations of water, heat, vibration and service handling. A lighting pigtail near a wet body panel may need a different seal and protection arrangement from a dry interior control lead. Document mounting location, connector orientation, drainage, harness support and likely chemical exposure. The goal is to select the right interface and protection for that location, not to apply the strongest-sounding material everywhere. A TE / AMP-based harness can connect several different families in one assembly when the equipment requires it; each end still needs its own documented mating reference and circuit definition.

11

Lead Time: Begin with a Complete BOM

An assembly schedule depends on the slowest approved input, not only the time required to cut and crimp wire. Check housing colour and key, contact plating, loose-piece or reel packaging, seals, special cable and tooling before agreeing a delivery date. A prototype using available components is not necessarily evidence that the same production BOM can be replenished at volume. Super Smile can review the requested quantity, sample milestones and material options with you; a confirmed quotation records the actual sourcing and approval assumptions. If an alternative becomes necessary, treat it as a controlled change with written approval rather than replacing a part solely to preserve a promised date.

12

Pricing: Define the Assembly, Not Just the Plug

A meaningful quotation separates recurring assembly cost from one-time development or tooling work. Recurring drivers include contact count, wire lengths, branch complexity, protection, labels, approved component sources, testing and packaging. One-time work may include drawing development, application tooling, test fixtures and first-article documentation. Part-number suffixes and plating choices can change the BOM even when the photograph looks identical. Send the mating references, pin table, dimensions, annual or batch quantity and delivery plan to obtain a comparable offer. We do not publish a universal TE / AMP harness price because it would hide the engineering differences between a short repair lead and a fully documented equipment loom.

TE / AMP Harness Questions

Does “TE / AMP compatible” mean every component is made by TE?

No. The quotation and approved BOM must state genuine specified parts and any separately approved alternatives. The category name identifies the interface requirement, not brand authorization or an unstated component origin.

Can a matching housing colour prove the connector will mate?

No. Compare full part numbers, family, keying, contact system and mating drawings. Colour may form part of a coding system, but is not sufficient identification by itself.

Can you develop an assembly from a sample?

An authorized sample can support dimensional and mechanical review. Circuit functions, material requirements, mating references and acceptance criteria still need confirmation before production.

Does an IP-rated connector make the entire harness waterproof?

No. The rating applies to a defined configuration and test condition. Other joints, cable entries, unused cavities and installation details require their own assessment.
