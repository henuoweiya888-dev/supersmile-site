About Aptiv / Delphi Wiring Harnesses

Aptiv-interface cable assemblies connect power, sensing and control functions across vehicle and equipment architectures. This category includes sealed branches, powertrain looms, extension leads and project-reviewed high-voltage assemblies. Super Smile develops the requested harness from drawings, samples or confirmed mating references. The approved BOM identifies original branded components and any separately proposed alternatives. The following guide moves from connector families to manufacturing, applications and platform routing so an enquiry can describe the complete assembly rather than a connector photograph alone.

01

Classic Connector Families

Aptiv is not a single mating interface. Weather-Pack, Metri-Pack and GT describe different connection systems. Metri-Pack includes sealed and unsealed arrangements; an unsealed M/P 150 housing must not inherit a waterproof claim from another family member. GT 150 and GT 280 are useful catalogue search terms, not permission to substitute terminals or housings. Identify the complete male and female references, cavity layout, keying, contact finish and lock accessories. Keep a photograph for recognition, but release production against a controlled drawing and bill of materials.

02

Product Series: Sealed Harnesses

A sealed branch is a system of matched housing, interface seal, wire seals, contacts and unused-cavity treatment. The seal grips the insulation outside diameter, while the crimp grips the conductor; specifying AWG alone does not settle both requirements. Weather-Pack or a specified sealed Metri-Pack arrangement may suit an exposed branch when its approved application conditions match the project. Define the mated state, rear support, fluid exposure and required test method. A dust cap or rubber boot does not by itself convert an unsealed connector into a qualified sealed assembly.

03

Product Series: Powertrain Harnesses

An engine and transmission loom combines circuits with very different jobs: temperature and pressure sensing, ignition control, solenoid actuation and auxiliary loads. Define each connection from the approved equipment interface rather than treating all branches alike. Routing near exhaust surfaces, moving pulleys or service covers requires local protection and deliberate support. Heat sleeve selection must consider the cable beneath it and the duration of exposure. Document breakout locations, connector orientation and permitted movement so the replacement assembly can be installed without pulling on contacts or obstructing maintenance.

04

Product Series: High-Voltage Harnesses

High-voltage work starts with a project review, not an orange cable. Aptiv ShieldPack references such as HV280 identify systems with specific contact, sealing, shielding and interlock options. The selected connector must be checked against voltage, current, conductor size, clearance, creepage and installation constraints. HVIL routing and shield termination belong in the drawing when required. Component literature does not certify the finished harness. High-voltage manufacture and verification are accepted only after the material specification, tooling, safety process and inspection scope have been confirmed for the project.

05

Product Series: Custom Extended Harnesses

Extending a cable changes more than its length. Additional conductor resistance affects voltage drop and heating; an added signal segment can also change capacitance, shielding continuity or communication behaviour. Define the original and new mating interfaces, pin-to-pin map, installed route and maximum load before choosing wire construction. Place service loops where they are supported, not where they can snag or chafe. For data circuits, obtain the protocol and channel requirements. A successful continuity test alone does not prove that an extended sensor or communications link will perform correctly in the equipment.

06

Custom Solutions for Vehicle Platforms

Passenger, commercial and special vehicles can share some connector families while requiring different harness designs. Start with the platform drawing, mounting points, variant list and service strategy. Separate common trunk wiring from optional branches so a missing feature does not leave an uncontrolled open circuit. Confirm the polarity and pin assignment even when two housings mate mechanically. Super Smile can review a defined assembly or replacement sub-harness against customer data; compatibility is tied to the approved configuration, not to a broad claim covering every vehicle carrying an Aptiv component.

07

Custom Solutions for Electric Vehicles

An electric platform contains low-voltage sensing and control as well as high-voltage power distribution. Keep these systems distinct in drawings, identification and verification. A battery-temperature branch, coolant-pump lead and traction-power connection do not use the same qualification assumptions. Specify grounding, shielding, interlock functions and the required connector coding where applicable. Assembly review should include the service disconnect sequence and the space needed to operate locks. We assess the requested cable assembly within the supplied vehicle architecture; battery safety or whole-vehicle certification cannot be inferred from one connector selection.

08

Custom Solutions for Industrial Equipment

A machine harness may join valve manifolds, limit switches, pumps, motor controllers and operator controls. Record which connections move and which remain fixed. The connector supports a serviceable interface, while clamps and cable construction manage mechanical loads along the route. Oil, cleaning agents, dust and repeated bending should be considered independently. For a replacement assembly, inspect the mating device and the installed branch geometry rather than copying a loose cable's relaxed shape. Drawings should identify the reference point for each length so installation and inspection use the same dimensions.

09

Aptiv and the Former Delphi Name

Delphi Automotive announced the Aptiv name for its mobility business in 2017 as part of the separation from Delphi Technologies. Legacy drawings, moulded markings and purchasing records may therefore use different names. A historical brand name is helpful for searching, but it is not a substitute for the complete part reference and current drawing revision. Cross-check superseded numbers, mating parts and terminal requirements in the manufacturer's catalogue. Keep the approved original part and any proposed alternative separate on the BOM. This page describes interface options and does not imply that Super Smile is an authorized Aptiv distributor.

10

Core Advantages: A Defined Connection System

The practical value of a custom harness is controlled fit and repeatability. Matched interfaces reduce assembly ambiguity; suitable seals and protection address the actual environment; correct conductor and contact choices support the electrical duty. Weight can be reduced through route planning rather than by removing necessary copper or protection. These benefits depend on engineering decisions and process control, not on a brand name alone. We document the mating system, circuit map, dimensions and inspection criteria so purchasing, assembly and maintenance teams work from the same agreed configuration.

11

Production: Controlled Termination

Release wire cutting and stripping only after the conductor, insulation and terminal specification is settled. Use the contact-specific application instruction to set strip length, crimp tooling and the acceptance criteria. Inspect strands for damage and verify conductor capture and insulation support where the terminal design provides it. After insertion, confirm cavity assignment and terminal retention before closing secondary locks. A similar-looking terminal can need a different applicator or setting. Keep tooling and drawing revisions linked to the job so repeat batches do not inherit an undocumented adjustment.

12

Quality Inspection Across the Assembly

Incoming checks identify the specified housings, terminals, seals and wire construction. In-process checks address strip quality, termination, cavity position and branch dimensions. Final electrical verification follows the agreed circuit map, including intended splices, blank cavities and any fitted components. Continuity is not a measurement of insulation resistance, sealing or communication performance. Additional tests must be specified with conditions and acceptance limits. Record the first-article results and agree how repeat production will be sampled or checked, rather than presenting a generic test list as evidence that every test has already been performed.

13

Compliance and Qualification Records

Separate component qualification, workmanship criteria and the finished assembly's project requirements. A manufacturer's rating applies to its stated configuration and test conditions, not automatically to a modified harness. Where a customer specifies standards such as IPC/WHMA-A-620 or an automotive connector test specification, agree the applicable revision, class, scope and evidence required before quoting. Material declarations and test records are different documents and should not be substituted for each other. We confirm which records are included with an order and which validation activities require a separately approved plan.

14

Industries: Passenger Vehicles

Cabin, body and engine-area branches have different exposure and packaging requirements. Lighting, seat functions, climate controls and sensors may use related connection technologies but require distinct pin maps and wire construction. Review the specific trim level and mating device before making a replacement. Secure the loom against trim edges and moving mechanisms, and preserve the intended service access. Airbag and other safety-related circuits require the customer's explicit approved design and verification route; a visually compatible connector is not sufficient authorization to alter them.

15

Industries: Commercial Trucks and Fleets

Long routes, cab movement, frame flex and frequent maintenance influence commercial-vehicle harnesses. Define the articulation points and avoid carrying cable weight through a connector latch. Distinguish power, lighting and communication branches even when they run in the same corridor. Labels should identify circuit function and revision so workshop replacement is controlled. Fleet commonality is useful only when vehicle variants have been checked. Keep a traceable record of the assembly version fitted to each application rather than promising one harness for every truck in a fleet.

16

Industries: New-Energy Vehicles

Electric vehicles need accurate low-voltage interfaces for battery sensing, thermal management and control modules alongside their traction-power architecture. State whether the requested assembly carries measurement signals, auxiliary power or high voltage. Shielding, isolation and interlock requirements follow that classification. A low-voltage sealed plug is not a high-voltage substitute. For prototype programmes, freeze the mating references and pin map before comparing sample feedback, so a wiring revision is not mistaken for a component performance change.

17

Industries: Machinery and Robotics

A fixed connection and a moving robot dress pack face different mechanical demands. Record bend radius, travel, torsion, cycle expectation and the space around each axis. The cable construction and support method must manage movement; the connector should not become the flex point. Keep electrical noise sources and sensitive measurement circuits in view when selecting routing and shielding. The applicable Aptiv interface is specified from the controller or device drawing. Dynamic suitability requires evaluation of the complete installed system, not only a terminal catalogue entry.

18

Industries: Marine and Off-Road Equipment

Salt, spray, mud and washdown are different exposures. A marine sensor lead may also face ultraviolet light and dissimilar-metal corrosion at mounting hardware. Match the chosen connection system, cable jacket and protective accessories to the specific installation and required validation. Locate service connectors above persistent water traps when the design allows it, and provide strain relief that does not damage the seal. A generic IP description does not establish salt-spray resistance or suitability for permanent immersion; those requirements need their own documented review.

19

Industries: Agricultural Equipment

Sprayers, irrigation equipment, harvesters and tractors can expose harnesses to dust, fertiliser, crop debris and relative movement between attachments. Define implement disconnect points, branch supports and the route through folding or lifting mechanisms. Chemical compatibility should be checked against the actual substance and concentration, not simply described as oil resistance. Clear cavity mapping and accessible locks make seasonal attachment changes more controlled. Specify the approved interface at both ends and any sensing or control requirements before treating two agricultural leads as interchangeable.

20

Lighter Harnesses Through Route Planning

Reducing unnecessary length can lower material use without reducing conductor size below the required electrical duty. Review branch topology, connector location and service allowance together. A shorter route is not better if it crosses a heat source or removes access needed for maintenance. Where alternative conductor materials or thinner insulation are proposed, review termination compatibility, voltage drop, thermal behaviour and mechanical durability before approval. We compare the defined options in the BOM and drawing; no percentage weight saving or vehicle range improvement is claimed without a measured baseline.

21

Tailored Routing for Each Vehicle Zone

Design review begins with the installation envelope. Chassis routes need clearance from suspension and steering movement; powertrain routes need local heat and vibration assessment. Interior branches must avoid seat tracks, trim fasteners and service panels. Underbody routes need protection against road debris and retained moisture. Mark clips, grommets, pass-throughs and inspection datums on the drawing rather than leaving them to assembly judgement. The same connector family can appear in several zones, but the cable, protection and verification requirements may be different in each one.

22

Systems Built for Platform Flexibility

A modular harness separates a shared trunk from defined optional branches. That can simplify servicing and variant management, provided the interfaces, pin assignments and protection remain controlled. An unused option needs a specified termination or cavity treatment. Adding a sensor or module also requires confirmation of the circuit capacity and communication architecture; an available connector position is not automatically a spare functional circuit. Use revision-controlled labels and a compatibility matrix in the engineering package so upgrades and replacement assemblies can be matched to the correct platform version.

23

Sourcing the Specified Harness

Send the full mating references, pin map, wire requirements, installed dimensions, environment and sample and production quantities. Identify whether original branded components are mandatory and whether alternatives may be proposed for approval. A quotation should separate assembly scope, tooling, documentation and any specialised validation. Availability of the housing alone does not establish availability of terminals, seals and locks. We confirm the sourcing and delivery plan for the approved BOM rather than advertising universal stock. The final release should connect the agreed drawing revision to the supplied assembly and inspection record.

Engineering and Procurement Questions

Does an Aptiv label establish compatibility?

No. Confirm the complete mating references, cavity arrangement, keying, contacts and pin map. A shared brand or shell colour is not sufficient.

Are all Metri-Pack connectors sealed?

No. The portfolio contains sealed and unsealed configurations. Specify the exact version and its application conditions.

Can an extension use the original wire size?

It requires review. Length affects resistance and voltage drop, while signal circuits can also have channel constraints. Confirm load and protocol requirements.

What is included in a production quotation?

The quote should identify the approved BOM, assembly quantity, tooling, included inspections and documentation. Special validation and unapproved alternatives are separately scoped.
