JAE Harnesses: Interfaces and Engineering

JAE wiring covers many constructions: non-sealed vehicle controls, sealed board connections, display cables and compact equipment interconnects. The right assembly starts with the actual mating parts and circuit requirements, not a generic brand label. This guide explains interface choices, materials, installation and validation so that procurement and engineering can define the same build. Super Smile reviews project-specific harnesses from drawings, samples and confirmed connector references.

01

JAE Connectors: Start with the Complete Interface

Japan Aviation Electronics Industry supplies many connector families, not one interchangeable interface. A JAE cable assembly can connect a PCB, display, sensor or removable equipment module. Identify the housing, contact, mating header and locking arrangement together. Super Smile reviews cable length, circuit mapping, protection and assembly instructions against that interface. Original-brand parts and proposed alternatives must be distinguished in the approved bill of materials.

02

MX34: Non-Waterproof Vehicle Wiring

MX34 is a high-density, non-waterproof automotive connector family. Board and in-line configurations support different packaging arrangements; straight and right-angle headers are not equivalent cable exits. In a body controller or protected switch assembly, verify connector access after the enclosure closes. Record cavity numbering from the specified mating view, terminal retention and wire dressing. A vehicle application alone does not establish water resistance.

03

MX23A: Sealing Is a Complete Assembly

MX23A waterproof board-to-cable connections use sealing features at both the mating interface and wire entry. The sealing ring, rear grommet and specified plugs for unused cavities are part of the system. Match wire outside diameter and front-cap configuration, not only conductor size and pin count. Assembly damage, an unfilled cavity or strain at the cable exit can compromise the intended seal. Agree the finished-assembly exposure and acceptance test separately.

04

IL-AG5: Retention and Service Access

The 2.5mm-pitch IL-AG5 family includes board-to-cable and cable-to-cable connections. Its terminal-retention and mechanical-locking features must be used with the correct components. Do not infer compatibility from pitch alone: housing shape, contact geometry and polarization also matter. Define where a technician can press the latch and pull the housing, without loading the wires. A service loop should help disconnection without allowing the harness to rub against nearby parts.

05

FI and FI-X Display Connections

FI and FI-X are separate connector families. JAE describes FI-X as a 1.0mm-pitch LCD interface with a metal shell and differential-transmission design. The cable still needs the correct lane assignment, grounds, shielding and termination. A connector catalogue impedance is not a measurement of the complete harness. Review the board connector drawing and display pinout together; similar-looking display plugs can place supply voltage on different positions.

06

Fine Pitch Is Not a Wire Specification

A small contact pitch can help fit more circuits into a compact board area, but it does not select the cable for you. The contact application specification determines allowable conductor and insulation dimensions. Check strip length, exposed strands and insulation support under suitable magnification. Board-to-board and FPC connectors should not be substituted for crimp-wire interfaces merely because the brand and pitch match.

07

Automotive Qualification: Read the Scope

AEC-Q is not a blanket certification for every automotive connector. Its document families address particular electronic-component types. For a connector project, request the exact part drawing, applicable connector test specification and report scope. Distinguish a component evaluation from a completed harness test and from vehicle approval. If a customer names USCAR-2 or another requirement, agree its revision, test groups and acceptance evidence before quoting compliance.

08

Shielding and the Signal Return Path

High-speed wiring depends on the full path through the cable, connector and board transition. Keep paired conductors together and control untwisted length where the design requires it. A metal shell is useful only when its bonding and cable-screen connection suit the system. Separate sensitive signal routing from switching power where practical. Continuity checks confirm connections, but they do not establish insertion loss, crosstalk or an acceptable eye diagram.

09

Industrial Automation and Robotics

Controller cabinets, encoders, sensors and removable robot modules need different harness constructions. A connector at a fixed control board does not make the attached cable suitable for continuous flexing. Separate the moving section from the fixed section, define the bend path and support transitions before they reach the connector. Supply the motion envelope, service access and expected environment so that cable construction and protection can be reviewed together.

10

Automotive and New-Energy Controls

Body electronics, auxiliary modules and low-voltage battery controls can require connector-specific branches and mounting points. These control circuits must not be confused with high-voltage traction wiring. Specify supply range, load behavior, fuse protection, installation zone and mating references. For repairs or retrofits, document changes to the original pinout rather than relying on wire colors; the same color may identify different functions in different vehicle versions.

11

Medical Equipment Interconnections

Displays, measurement boards and equipment modules may use compact connector assemblies. Review electrical separation, cleaning exposure, strain relief and service replacement against the equipment design. Internal wiring is not automatically suitable for direct patient contact, sterilization or fluid exposure. The equipment manufacturer must define the intended use and validation requirements. We review those requirements before proposing materials or a production inspection plan.

12

Contact Alloys: Do Not Generalize

Connector contacts balance conductivity, spring force, forming behavior and wear. Copper alloy, phosphor bronze and beryllium copper are not interchangeable material descriptions. Do not assume every JAE terminal is beryllium copper. The selected part drawing controls the alloy and finish; for example, JAE documentation includes copper-alloy contacts in IL-AG5 products. Replacement decisions also need contact geometry, mating force and the approved crimp-wire combination.

13

LCP for Compact Connector Housings

Liquid crystal polymer can support thin-wall moulding and precise small features in suitable grades. That makes it relevant to compact connector design, but not proof that every JAE housing is LCP. JAE also specifies other resins for other parts. Review the exact grade, processing conditions and operating exposure. Soldering heat resistance and long-term service temperature are different questions, and a resin family name alone does not answer either.

14

Glass-Filled Polyamide and PBT

Glass reinforcement can improve stiffness and dimensional behavior, while moisture conditioning affects polyamide properties. PA66-GF and PBT are distinct choices, not generic substitutes. Some JAE IL-AG5 documentation specifies PBT, so the material must be checked by part rather than copied from a broad brand description. Confirm chemical exposure, latch flexibility, thermal conditions and material declarations when reviewing the housing and any proposed substitute.

15

Plating and Mating Surfaces

Gold over nickel and tin finishes appear in different connector specifications. Their thickness, coverage and mating surface must match the intended contact system. A gold-colored part is not evidence of the required plating. Avoid substituting contacts solely by appearance, and keep oils, abrasive cleaning and uncontrolled rework away from the mating surface. The approved BOM should preserve the complete contact suffix and finish option.

16

Consumer and Embedded Electronics

A display, camera or compact computing module may need a short harness that folds through a narrow enclosure. Establish the connector height, cable exit and fold location before fixing the overall length. Check that a cover screw or sharp chassis edge cannot trap the cable. Small assemblies benefit from clear orientation marks and installation instructions, particularly when the two cable ends look similar but carry different pin assignments.

17

Telecom and Network Equipment

Rack equipment combines power distribution, control wiring and data paths in limited space. Identify each circuit class and its connector rather than treating all internal cables as equivalent. Plan access for module replacement and cooling-air paths, and avoid placing a service loop in a fan inlet. Signal-integrity requirements, shielding and cable length should follow the actual interface specification, not a speed implied by the connector brand.

18

Aerospace and Defence Project Review

A manufacturer name associated with aviation does not qualify every connector or harness for flight hardware. Such projects can involve controlled drawings, traceability, environmental testing and specific material or process approvals. State the equipment role and required evidence at the enquiry stage. We distinguish an engineering feasibility review from an approved supply commitment; no blanket aerospace, military or flightworthiness qualification is implied.

19

Continuity and Miswire Inspection

A test program should be built from the approved netlist, including intentional connections and circuits that must remain separate. Check opens, shorts and crossed pins against that reference, not against an unverified sample. Fixture wear and contact condition can affect measurements, so retain the tester setup and fixture identity with the work instructions. A passing continuity result does not prove the cable can carry its design current without excessive heating.

20

Dielectric Withstand Testing

A dielectric withstand test stresses specified insulation barriers under an agreed voltage, duration and trip criterion. It is not the operating voltage rating and should not be applied indiscriminately to assemblies containing electronics. Identify connected components, grounded screens and test groups first. The customer-approved plan determines whether this is a qualification, sampling or production test, and how the assembly is made safe after the test.

21

Insulation Resistance and Cleanliness

Insulation resistance is measured between circuits intended to be isolated at a defined test voltage and settling time. Moisture, contamination and fixture leakage can influence the result. Specify the measurement conditions and acceptance threshold instead of writing only “insulation tested”. Keep this check distinct from continuity and dielectric withstand. For sensitive signal assemblies, cleanliness and handling controls may be as important as the nominal insulation material.

22

Longevity Begins with Installation

Connector life depends on mating cycles, temperature, vibration, contamination and load, not simply on brand selection. Route the cable so that clamps carry installation loads before they reach the terminals. Avoid sharp bends at the rear of a housing and provide protection at panel edges. Define service inspection and replacement criteria for the actual equipment. Catalogue durability data applies to stated conditions and is not a universal years-in-service promise.

23

Application-Specific Pinout and Length

Specify the connector view, cavity numbers, signal names and wire identification at both ends. Define the dimension datum: housing face, rear exit or another drawing reference. Include branch lengths, tolerance, sleeve positions and label direction where installation depends on them. A controlled pinout makes sample approval repeatable and allows later revisions to be compared without guessing from photographs or wire colors.

24

Contacts, Wire and Crimp Tooling

JAE publishes tooling references by contact and series. Select the approved terminal, wire range and tool combination together; a tool that closes the barrel is not necessarily suitable. Define preparation, inspection and any agreed destructive sample checks in the process plan. Terminal insertion should confirm retention without damaging the lance. Rework requires an appropriate extraction method and a clear rule for replacing damaged contacts or housings.

25

Sourcing and Revision Control

Record the original manufacturer, full part number and approved alternative status in the BOM. Where traceability is required, agree supplier documentation and lot identification before purchasing. Availability changes should trigger a review, not a silent substitution. Keep the released drawing, sample approval and inspection plan aligned so that a later batch reproduces the agreed configuration rather than merely resembling the first sample.

Selection and Ordering Questions

Does compatible mean an original JAE connector?

Not by itself. The approved BOM must distinguish original JAE parts from any separately reviewed alternative. Compatibility needs the exact mating reference and requirements.

Are MX34 and MX23A both waterproof?

No. MX34 is non-waterproof. MX23A uses a sealing system whose wire entry, mating components and unused cavities must be correctly assembled.

Can I order by pin count and pitch?

Those are useful starting details, but not sufficient. Provide the complete part number, mating view, contact and wire specification, and a verified pinout.

Can you customize branches and wire lengths?

Yes, subject to design review. Drawings should define length datums, tolerances, branch positions, mounting and circuit mapping.

Does continuity testing prove high-speed performance?

No. Continuity checks circuit connections. Signal-integrity verification requires agreed measurements and limits for the actual interface and cable length.

What should accompany an existing sample?

Add the mating part references, confirmed electrical functions, dimensions, environment and required tests. A sample alone may not reveal hidden circuit details or the intended operating limits.
