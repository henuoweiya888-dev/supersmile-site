Product Introduction

Fast engineering learning with controlled revisions

Prototype and small-batch harnesses cover proof-of-concept samples, fit checks, functional verification, pilot equipment, replacement trials and recurring low-volume builds. A sample may prove one purpose while other dimensions, materials or processes remain provisional. We therefore record the test objective, current revision, assumptions, temporary substitutions and open points, then convert the findings into a controlled drawing, BOM and inspection plan before repeat production.

Prototype and Small-Batch Types

Proof-of-concept harness

Used to verify a circuit idea, interface choice or basic function before the mechanical package is frozen. Provisional dimensions and substitutions are marked clearly.

Fit and interface sample

Focuses on connector mating, branch position, routing, clips, bend space and service access. Electrical load testing may remain outside this sample's approved purpose.

Functional engineering sample

Built with defined pin mapping and agreed materials to validate continuity, polarity, signal or equipment operation under a stated test setup.

Pilot-build harness

A small controlled batch checks assembly sequence, work instructions, tooling, inspection points and variation before a wider production release.

Replacement and reverse-development sample

A physical reference can support measurement and circuit mapping, but mating interfaces, hidden components, ratings and intended function still require customer confirmation.

Recurring low-volume harness

For service parts, specialist equipment and low annual demand, the approved configuration is reproduced in manageable batches with revision and material control.

Material and Build Recommendations

Production-intent conductors where critical

Current, voltage drop, temperature, flexing and termination determine which conductors must match the intended production design from the first functional sample.

Document temporary material substitutions

When a specified jacket, colour or accessory is unavailable, the substitute, reason, affected test and replacement plan are recorded rather than silently accepted as final.

Use representative terminals and splices

Crimp geometry, conductor range, seals and splice construction influence both fit and electrical behavior, so critical interfaces should be production representative.

Separate sample appearance from qualification

A neat sealed connector does not by itself prove ingress, temperature or endurance. Materials and tests are tied to the stated sample purpose and final installation.

Development Solutions

Start from incomplete engineering data

We can organize drawings, pin tables, photos, marked samples and equipment layouts into a review list that separates confirmed requirements from assumptions.

Build for one defined test purpose

Fit, function, environmental and production-process samples answer different questions. Defining the purpose avoids unnecessary cost and misleading acceptance.

Convert feedback into controlled revisions

Every approved change updates the applicable drawing, BOM, pin map, label or inspection requirement so the next build does not depend on memory.

Bridge prototype to repeat production

After design review, production-intent materials, tooling, work instructions and inspection points replace provisional methods before the configuration is released.

Prototype Harness Making Method

  1. Purpose and data review

    Confirm what the sample must prove, current revision, open dimensions, must-use parts, acceptable substitutions, quantity and target review date.

  2. Engineering definition

    Create or refine circuit mapping, branch datums, connector views, labels, protection, assumptions and acceptance points.

  3. Material and process review

    Identify production-intent items, temporary parts, manual operations, tooling gaps and which results may not transfer directly to volume production.

  4. Controlled sample build

    Build to the reviewed data and record deviations, substitutions, rework and actual dimensions instead of correcting them invisibly.

  5. Fit, function and inspection

    Perform the agreed checks only, record setup and results, then distinguish accepted requirements from items that remain open.

  6. Revision release and next build

    Convert approved feedback into the next drawing and BOM revision; repeat builds start from that released configuration.

Validation and Revision Review

Test purpose and specimen limits

A result applies to the stated sample, setup and acceptance rule. Temporary materials or incomplete sealing can limit what the result proves.

Mechanical fit versus endurance

Passing a fit check does not establish bend, vibration or fatigue life. Endurance needs representative routing, fixation and a defined cycle.

Environmental representativeness

Jackets, seals, braid and labels used in environmental samples must represent the intended configuration or be recorded as limitations.

Electrical checks and change impact

Continuity, polarity, isolation, resistance or other tests follow the approved scope; wiring changes trigger review of affected checks.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much information is needed to start a prototype?

A drawing is helpful but not mandatory. A sample, pin table, connector references, photos and equipment layout can start review when the intended test purpose and open points are stated.

Can temporary materials be used?

They may be used when the customer accepts the substitution and its effect on fit, function or testing is recorded. Temporary parts are not silently converted into the production BOM.

Does a working sample mean the design is ready for mass production?

No. A functional sample may still use manual methods, provisional dimensions or unqualified materials. Production release requires a controlled design, repeatable process and agreed inspection plan.

Can you build from an existing sample?

Yes, as a development reference. Hidden electrical functions, mating interfaces, ratings, approved materials and intellectual-property responsibility still need customer confirmation.

How are design changes controlled?

Changes are linked to a drawing, BOM, pin map or review record revision. The next build uses the released revision and does not combine undocumented instructions.

Can prototypes continue as low-volume production?

Yes after the configuration, substitutions, tooling, work instructions and inspections are reviewed and the customer approves the production reference.
